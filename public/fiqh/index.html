Book of Purification (Kitāb al-Ṭahārah)

Purification, Impure Substances & Categories

Understanding taharah is the foundation of all worship in Islam. This chapter covers the linguistic and Islamic definitions of purification and its subcategories.

📖

Definition of Ṭahārah (Purification)

📚 Linguistic Meaning

Ṭahārah in Arabic means cleanliness and keeping away from dirt and filth. It refers to the state of being clean and free from physical and metaphorical impurities.

Type 1 — Metaphorical (Maʿnawī) Purification

Refers to purifying the heart and keeping it free of shirk (polytheism) and sin. This is an inner, spiritual purification.

Type 2 — Physical (Ḥissī) Purification

Refers to removal of metaphorical impurity (hadath) through wudu/ghusl, and removal of physical impurities (najasah) from body, clothes and place of prayer.

«O you who have believed, indeed the polytheists are unclean [najis]»
[at-Tawbah 9:28]
«Allah intends only to remove from you the impurity [of sin], O people of the [Prophet's] household, and to purify you with [extensive] purification»
[al-Ahzab 33:33]
🔵

Types of Metaphorical Impurity (Ḥadath)

Ḥadath is a metaphorical/legal impurity. It is divided into two types:

Minor Ḥadath (Ḥadath Aṣghar)

A metaphorical impurity affecting four body parts: the face, arms, head and feet. It prevents prayer and similar actions from being valid. Removed by doing wudu.

Major Ḥadath (Ḥadath Akbar)

A metaphorical impurity affecting the entire body, preventing prayer and all similar actions from being valid. Removed by doing ghusl (full body wash).

✅ The distinction between minor and major hadath is fundamental — it determines whether wudu or ghusl is required to restore purity.
🌳

Full Categories of Purification (Summary Chart)

Purification branches into two main types, each with further subcategories:

Metaphorical Purification

• From minor hadath → Wudu
• From major hadath → Ghusl

Physical Purification

From najasah (impure substances) on:
• The body
• One's clothes
• The place where one prays

⚠️

Impure Substances (Najasah)

Najasah refers to physical impurities that must be removed from the body, clothing, and place of prayer. The word khabath also refers to such physical impurities.

Examples of najasah include: urine, feces, blood (in larger amounts), alcohol, pork, and dead animals not slaughtered according to Islamic law.

⚖️ Key Ruling

Physical impurities must be removed from the body, clothing, and place of prayer for worship to be valid. Failure to remove najasah renders prayer invalid.

Two Types of Najasah:

Severe (Mughallaẓah)

Requires a specific cleaning method — e.g., from the saliva of a dog: the item must be washed seven times, one of which is with earth/soil.

Light (Mukhaffafah)

Such as the urine of an infant boy who has not yet eaten food. Sufficient to sprinkle water over the affected area without scrubbing.

Water (Māʾ) & Its Categories

Water is the primary agent of purification in Islam. This chapter covers the classification of water and the rulings on each type.

💧

The Two Main Categories of Water

📚 General Rule

In general terms, water is divided into two categories: Pure Water (Māʾ Ṭāhir) and Impure Water (Māʾ Najis).

1. Pure Water (Ṭāhir)

Water that remains as Allah created it — has NOT been mixed with any impure substance that changes its taste, colour or smell.

2. Impure Water (Najis)

Water that has been mixed with an impure substance so that its colour, taste or smell has changed. It is forbidden to use for purification, drinking or cooking.

🌊

Category 1: Pure Water — Details & Rulings

Pure water is water that remains the way Allah created it and has not been mixed with any impure substance causing a change in its taste, colour or smell.

💡 Examples: Rain water, river water, spring water, sea water, well water — any water that comes down from the sky or comes up from the earth in its natural state.
⚖️ Ruling on Pure Water

It removes ḥadath (metaphorical impurity) and removes najasah (physical impure substances). It is valid for wudu, ghusl and removing najasah.

📿 Evidence — Seawater

«Its water is pure and its 'dead meat' [animals that die without being slaughtered in the prescribed manner] are halal.»

Narrated by Malik in al-Muwatta; classed as sahih by al-Albani
«and We send down from the sky pure water»
[al-Furqan 25:48]

Pure Water That is NOT Affected by Changes:

Water remains pure even if it changes due to:

  • Standing for a long period of time
  • What is in the vicinity (surrounding substances)
  • Things that are difficult to protect it from, such as leaves from trees, water moss and the like

Water Changed by Pure Substances — Three Scenarios:

  • Scenario 1: Pure substance does NOT change water's colour, taste or smell → Water remains pure, because it is still called water.
  • Scenario 2: Pure substance changes water so much it is no longer called water (e.g. tea, juice, broth) → NOT valid for purification, because it has become something else.
  • Scenario 3: Pure substance changes some characteristics but it is still called water (e.g. soap, saffron, chickpeas) → The most correct scholarly view is that it may still be used for purification.
✅ Water used to remove hadath (by doing wudu or ghusl) is pure in and of itself and may be used again for purification according to the correct scholarly view.
🚫

Category 2: Impure Water (Māʾ Najis)

📚 Definition

Impure (najis) water is water that has been mixed with an impure substance, so its colour, taste or smell has changed.

⚖️ Ruling

It is NOT permissible to use impure water for purification, drinking or cooking food.

Ways to Purify Impure Water:

  • Adding more water to the impure water until the changes that made it impure are undone (colour, taste and smell return to normal).
  • Letting it stand for a long time — if the three characteristics of impurity disappear with the passage of time, the water is deemed pure.
  • Treating the water with modern technology, as happens in sewage treatment plants. No matter what means is used to remove impurity, it becomes pure again.

Impure Substances Falling into Water — Three Scenarios:

Scenario 1 — Changes characteristics

The impurity changes colour, taste or smell → water is impure (najis) according to unanimous scholarly opinion, regardless of amount.

Scenario 2 — Large water, no change

Impurity falls into a large amount and does not change any characteristic → water remains pure by unanimous agreement.

Scenario 3 — Small water, no change

Impurity falls into a small amount and does not change characteristics → scholars differ. The most correct view: it is pure, based on the hadith «Water is pure and nothing makes it impure.» (Narrated by at-Tirmidhi; classed as sahih by al-Albani)

📿 Key Principle

«Water is pure and nothing makes it impure.»

Narrated by at-Tirmidhi; classed as sahih by al-Albani. Scholars are unanimously agreed that water becomes impure ONLY if one of its characteristics changes.

Vessels (Awāni) — Rulings

Islamic law has specific rulings on which vessels (containers) may be used for eating, drinking and purification.

🫙

Definition & General Rule

📚 Definition

Vessels (awāni) are containers in which water and other things are kept. The basic principle is that all vessels are permissible to use — except those made of gold and silver.

💡 Expensive vessels other than gold and silver (e.g., rubies, diamonds) are permissible, because there is no text indicating they are prohibited.
🥇

Gold & Silver Vessels — Ruling

⚖️ Ruling — Gold & Silver Vessels

It is haram (forbidden) to use vessels of gold or silver for eating, drinking, purification, and all other uses. This applies equally to men and women.

📿 Evidence

«Do not drink from vessels of gold and silver, and do not eat from plates made of them, because they are for them [the disbelievers] in this world and for us in the Hereafter.»

Narrated by al-Bukhari and Muslim from Hudhayfah
📿 Additional Evidence

«The one who drinks from a vessel of silver is only imbibing into his belly the fire of Hell.»

Agreed upon (Bukhari & Muslim) from Umm Salamah

Vessels Soldered with Gold or Silver:

Soldered with Gold

Forbidden to use in any way whatsoever, because it comes under the general meaning of the prohibition text.

Soldered with Silver (small amount)

Permissible if the soldering is very small and there is a need for it — based on the hadith of Anas that the Prophet's cup broke and he repaired it with a silver strip.

✅ Is purification done with water from a gold/silver vessel valid? Yes — according to the most correct scholarly view, it is valid, because there is no connection between purification and the prohibition on such vessels. The vessel is not a condition of wudu.
🌍

Vessels of Disbelievers (Non-Muslims)

Category 1 — Not known to use impure substances

Their vessels are permissible. Evidence: The companions used to go on campaigns with the Prophet ﷺ and make use of the polytheists' vessels, and he did not criticise them for it. Also, the Prophet ﷺ ate in the house of a Jewish woman.

Category 2 — Known to use impure substances

Those who eat pork and drink alcohol from their vessels. It is better not to use them, but if no alternative exists, they should be washed before use.

📿 Evidence for Category 2

«If you can find something else, then do not eat from them, but if you cannot find anything else, then wash them and eat from them.»

Agreed upon — Hadith of Abu Tha'labah al-Khushani

Istinjāʾ & Sunan al-Fiṭrah

This chapter covers the etiquettes of relieving oneself and the natural practices (Sunan al-Fitrah) that are part of the Islamic way of life.

🌿

Istinjāʾ — Cleaning After Relieving Oneself

📚 Definition

Istinjāʾ means removing the impurity (najasah) from the front or back passage after relieving oneself. It can be done with water, or with solid cleansing materials (istitjābar), or both.

Methods of Istinjāʾ:

  • Water alone: The most complete and thorough method. The impurity is washed away until clean.
  • Solid materials (istijmār): Using stones, toilet paper or similar materials to wipe away the impurity. Must be done at least three times, or until clean.
  • Combination of both: Using stones/paper first, then washing with water — this is the most complete method.
⚖️ Conditions for Solid Materials

The material must be pure (tahir), it must remove the impurity, and it must not be something that is respected (such as food, bones, or anything that has the right of respect). Bones are prohibited because they are food for the jinn.

✨

Sunan al-Fiṭrah — Natural Practices of Purity

📚 Why "Fitrah"?

These practices are called Sunan al-Fitrah because the one who does them has the natural inclination (fitrah) which Allah has instilled in people and which He approves for them, so that they will have the best appearance and be in the best shape.

  • 1
    Using the Siwāk (tooth-cleaning stick)

    Using a stick (like the Arak tree) to remove food particles and dirt from the teeth. Using the siwak is Sunnah at all times. It is especially recommended when doing wudu, waking from sleep, when breath changes smell, before prayer, and when entering the home or mosque.

  • 2
    Shaving/Removing the Pubic Hair

    Refers to hair around the private part. It is elegant and clean to remove it. May be removed by shaving or other methods (e.g. hair removal cream).

  • 3
    Plucking/Removing Armpit Hair

    Removing armpit hair is part of cleanliness and hygiene as instructed by the Prophet ﷺ.

  • 4
    Trimming the Moustache

    The moustache should be trimmed and kept short so that food and drink do not touch it.

  • 5
    Clipping the Nails

    Fingernails and toenails should be kept trimmed for cleanliness and hygiene.

    • 📿 Time Limit for Sunan al-Fitrah

    «A time limit has been set for us to trim the moustache, clip the nails, pluck the armpit hair and shave the pubic hair — that should not be left for more than forty days.»

    Narrated by Muslim — from Anas ibn Malik
    📿 Hadith on Siwāk

    «The siwak is cleansing to the mouth and pleasing to the Lord.»

    Narrated by an-Nasai; classed as sahih by al-Albani
    📿 Hadith on Siwāk & Prayer

    «Were it not that it would be too difficult for my ummah, I would have instructed them to use the siwak at the time of every prayer.»

    Narrated by Muslim

    Relieving Oneself & Its Etiquette

    This chapter covers the Islamic rulings on relieving oneself, including istinjāʾ, istijmār, and the etiquettes that every Muslim must follow.

    📖

    Istinjāʾ & Istijmār — Definitions

    Istinjāʾ

    Removing traces of dirt from the front or back passage with water.

    Istijmār

    Removing traces of dirt from the front or back passage with pebbles, tissue paper, cloth and the like.

    💡 It is Sunnah to use an odd number of pebbles/materials for istijmār, because the Prophet ﷺ said: «Whoever cleans himself [with pebbles and the like], let him use an odd number.» (Agreed upon)
    ⚖️ Important Ruling

    If water is not available, it becomes obligatory to clean with pebbles, tissues and the like — traces of dirt from both the front and back passages.

    ✅

    Etiquette of Relieving Oneself (5 Adāb)

    • 1
      Say the du'a when entering the bathroom

      One should say: «Bismillah, Allahumma inni a'oodhu bika min al-khubthi wa'l-khabaa'ith» (In the name of Allah, O Allah, I seek refuge with You from the male and female devils.)

      📿 Evidence

      «The screen between the eyes of the jinn and the awrahs of the sons of Adam when one of them enters the bathroom is his saying, Bismillah.»

      Narrated by at-Tirmidhi; classed as sahih by al-Albani. Also: Al-Bukhari and Muslim from Anas narrate the full du'a.
    • 2
      Say the du'a when exiting the bathroom

      One should say: «Ghufranak» (I seek Your forgiveness) upon exiting.

      📿 Evidence

      Hadith of Aisha: When the Prophet ﷺ came out of the outhouse, he would say «Ghufranak.»

      Narrated by at-Tirmidhi; classed as sahih by al-Albani

      Also narrated from Abu Dharr: «Praise be to Allah who has taken away from me that which is harmful, and has granted me wellbeing.» (Narrated by Ibn Abi Shaybah; hasan)

    • 3
      Enter with the left foot first, exit with the right foot

      In accordance with the Islamic principle of giving precedence to the right-hand side in that which is honourable, and the left-hand side in the opposite.

    • 4
      Use the left hand when cleaning oneself

      The Prophet ﷺ said: «When one of you urinates, let him not touch his penis with his right hand, and when he goes to the outhouse, let him not wipe himself with his right hand.» (Agreed upon)

      Also required: Conceal one's awrah from people's sight — «Guard your awrah.» (Narrated by at-Tirmidhi; hasan by al-Albani)

    • 5
      Urinate in a sitting posture (recommended)

      It is mustahabb (recommended) to urinate whilst sitting, because that is what the Prophet ﷺ usually did, and because it is more concealing and safer in terms of preventing urine splashing back onto the body or clothes.

      ✅ If a person urinates whilst standing and there is no risk of contamination or the awrah being seen, there is nothing wrong with it.
    🚫

    What is Prohibited When Relieving Oneself

    ⚖️ Haram — Prohibited

    It is haram to urinate or defecate in the road, in the shade, in parks, beneath fruit-bearing trees, in waterways or in any place where that could cause annoyance or offence to people.

    📿 Evidence

    «Beware of the two things that incur curses» — «The one who relieves himself in the road where people walk or in the places where they seek shade.»

    Narrated by Muslim — Hadith of Abu Hurayrah

    Facing or Turning One's Back to the Qiblah:

    In the open desert — Prohibited

    It is NOT permissible to face the qiblah or turn one's back towards it when relieving oneself in the open without any barrier.

    Inside a building — Permitted (though better to avoid)

    If there is a wall or barrier between you and the qiblah, there is nothing wrong with it. Ibn Umar reported seeing the Prophet ﷺ sitting facing Syria with his back to the qiblah on the roof of Hafsah's house. (Muslim)

    📿 Evidence (Open land)

    «When you go to relieve yourselves, do not face the qiblah and do not turn your back towards it; rather face east or west.»

    Agreed upon — Hadith of Abu Ayub al-Ansari
    💡 It is better not to face the qiblah even inside buildings, and Muslims should pay attention to this when constructing buildings.
    ⚠️

    What is Disliked (Makrūh) When Relieving Oneself

    • Facing into the wind without a barrier, lest the wind blow the urine back onto you.
    • Speaking whilst relieving oneself, whether remembering Allah or otherwise. The Prophet ﷺ said: «No two men should go out to relieve themselves, uncovering their awrahs and talking to one another, for Allah detests that.» (Abu Dawud; sahih by al-Albani)
    • Urinating into a hole in the ground. The Prophet ﷺ forbade urinating into a hole (Abu Dawud; sahih by an-Nawawi) — because one cannot be sure there is no animal in the hole that could be harmed, or it may be the home of the jinn.
    • Entering the toilet with anything in which Allah is mentioned (e.g. a ring with Allah's name), except in cases of necessity. The Mushaf (Quran) is even more restricted — it is haram to take it in, whether visible or wrapped up, because it is the word of Allah and the noblest of speech.
    💡 Tapes and CDs with Quran recordings do not come under the same ruling as the Mushaf, on condition that the Quranic verses are not displayed on a screen.

    Sunan al-Fiṭrah — Natural Practices of Purity

    The Sunan al-Fitrah are natural practices instilled by Allah that every Muslim should observe as part of their cleanliness and overall appearance.

    📖

    Definition & Overview

    📚 Why "Fiṭrah"?

    They are called the characteristics of the fiṭrah because the one who does them has the fiṭrah (natural inclination) which Allah has instilled in people and which He approves for them, so that they will have the best appearance and be in the best shape.

    📿 Hadith of Abu Hurayrah — 5 of the Fitrah

    «Five things are part of the fitrah: circumcision, shaving the pubic hair, plucking the armpit hair, clipping the nails and trimming the moustache.»

    Agreed upon
    📿 Hadith of Aisha — 10 of the Fitrah

    «Ten things are part of the fitrah: trimming the moustache, letting the beard grow, using the siwak, taking water up into the nose, clipping the nails, washing the knuckles, plucking the armpit hair, shaving the pubic hair, and washing oneself with water» — referring to istinja. (The tenth, as Musab ibn Shaybah said, is rinsing the mouth with water.)

    Narrated by Muslim
    ⚖️ Time Limit

    It is makrūh to leave any of these practices for more than forty days, based on the hadith of Anas ibn Malik (Narrated by Muslim).

    ✨

    The 8 Sunan al-Fiṭrah in Detail

    • 1
      Using the Siwāk (tooth-cleaning stick)

      Refers to using a stick or the like as a toothbrush to remove food particles and dirt that cling to the teeth. Using the siwak is Sunnah at all times, because the Prophet ﷺ encouraged it in general terms without limiting it to a particular time.

      📿 Evidence

      «The siwak is cleansing to the mouth and pleasing to the Lord.»

      Narrated by an-Nasai; classed as sahih by al-Albani
      📿 Evidence 2

      «Were it not that it would be too difficult for my ummah, I would have instructed them to use the siwak at the time of every prayer.»

      Narrated by Muslim
      💡 Using the siwak is more important when doing wudu, waking from sleep, when the smell of the mouth changes, when wanting to read Quran and pray, and when entering the mosque or the home.
    • 2
      Shaving the Pubic Hair

      Refers to the hair that grows around the private part. Removing it is more elegant and clean. It may be removed in ways other than shaving, such as using hair removal creams.

    • 3
      Trimming and Cutting the Moustache

      Means cutting the moustache very short, because that is more elegant and more clean, and it is a way of differing from the disbelievers.

    • 4
      Letting the Beard Grow

      There are sahih hadiths which say that it is obligatory to let the beard grow and take good care of it, because maintaining the beard is more handsome and is a sign of manhood.

    • 5
      Clipping the Nails

      Refers to cutting the nails and not letting them grow long. Clipping makes them look more elegant and removes the dirt that may accumulate under them.

    • 6
      Plucking the Armpit Hair

      It is Sunnah to remove this hair by plucking, shaving or otherwise, because removing it is cleaner and stops the bad smells that may accumulate when this hair is present.

    • 7
      Washing the Knuckles

      Refers to the joints on the back of the fingers where dirt may accumulate. These should be washed thoroughly during wudu and ghusl.

    • 8
      Circumcision

      This is purifying and cleansing and has many virtues. It is one of the sunnahs of the fitrah. Added to these characteristics are: taking up water into the nose to clean it, rinsing the mouth with water, and cleaning oneself with water after relieving oneself (istinja).

    Wudu — Definition, Conditions & Obligatory Acts

    Wudu is the ritual purification required before prayer, tawaf, touching the Mushaf, and other acts of worship. This chapter covers everything about what makes wudu valid.

    📖

    Definition of Wudu

    Linguistic Meaning

    The word wudu is derived from the word al-wadaa'ah, which refers to beauty and cleanliness.

    Islamic Terminology

    Wudu refers to using water to wash the four parts — namely the face, arms, head and feet — in the manner specified in Islamic teachings, as an act of worship for Allah.

    ⚖️ Ruling on Wudu

    It is obligatory for the one who is in a state of impurity when he wants to pray and do other actions that come under the same ruling as prayer, such as tawaf (circumambulation of the Kaaba) and touching the Mushaf.

    «O you who have believed, when you rise to [perform] prayer, wash your faces and your forearms to the elbows and wipe over your heads and wash your feet to the ankles»
    [al-Māʾidah 5:6]
    📋

    Conditions for Wudu to be Valid (6 Conditions)

    For wudu to be valid, the following six things are stipulated:

    • Being Muslim — wudu is not valid if done by a disbeliever.
    • Being of sound mind — wudu does not count if done by one who is insane.
    • Having reached the age of discernment — it does not count if done by a child below the age of discernment.
    • Having the intention (niyyah) to do it, because of the hadith «Actions are but by intentions.» Note: It is not prescribed to utter the intention aloud, because there is no proven report from the Prophet ﷺ to suggest that he did that.
    • Using pure water — it is not valid to do wudu with impure water.
    • Removal of anything that may prevent water from reaching the skin, such as wax, dough, paint, nail polish and the like.
    💡 Do dyes, creams and lotions prevent water? In most cases, these things do not have any physical mass that forms a barrier, so they do not affect the validity of wudu or ghusl. However, if they DO have physical mass that prevents water reaching the skin, they must be removed before wudu.
    📿 Important Rule — Fake nails & nail polish

    A woman who is wearing fake nails must remove them when doing wudu, because they prevent water from reaching that part of the body. A woman who has put nail polish on her nails must remove it before doing wudu, as it prevents water reaching the skin. It is also not permissible to wear fake nails in the first place.

    Scholarly consensus based on the general condition of water reaching the skin
    💡 Fake eyelashes: Most forms of fake eyelashes prevent water from reaching that part of the face, therefore wudu is not valid if one is wearing them. It is also essential to point out that it is not permissible to wear them.
    💧

    The 6 Obligatory Acts (Farāʾiḍ) of Wudu

    If any obligatory act is omitted, the wudu is invalid.

    • 1
      Washing the Face in its Entirety

      Because Allah says: «wash your faces» [al-Ma'idah 5:6]. This also includes rinsing the mouth and nose, because the mouth and nose are part of the face.

      Rinsing the mouth (madmadah)

      Means moving water around in the mouth.

      Rinsing the nose (istinshāq)

      Means inhaling water into the nose, then expelling it from the nose.

    • 2
      Washing the Arms Including the Hands up to the Elbows

      Because Allah says: «and your forearms to the elbows» [al-Ma'idah 5:6]. The elbows must be included.

    • 3
      Wiping the Entire Head Including the Ears

      Because Allah says: «and wipe over your heads» [al-Ma'idah 5:6]. The Sunnah when wiping the head is as described in the hadith of Abdullah ibn Zayd: He wiped his head with both hands, from front to back, beginning at the front of the head until he reached the nape of the neck, then he brought his hands back to the place from which he had started. (Agreed upon)

      💡 A woman does not have to wipe the full length of her hair. One who is shaven-headed or bald should wipe from the original hairline to the nape of the neck. A person with hair implants may wipe over them.
    • 4
      Washing the Feet up to the Ankles

      Because Allah says: «and wash your feet to the ankles» [al-Ma'idah 5:6]. The ankles are the two bones that protrude at the bottom of the leg where it joins the foot. This does not refer to the back of the foot as some people think.

      📿 Warning

      «Woe to the heels from the Fire!» — indicating the importance of ensuring water reaches the heels and all parts of the foot including the ankles, elbows, between fingers and toes, and edges of the face.

      Narrated in the books of hadith
    • 5
      Doing These Actions in Order (Tartīb)

      Because in His Book, Allah mentioned the parts that are to be washed in a particular order, and everyone who described the wudu of the Prophet ﷺ described it in the same order. If any part is washed out of order, the wudu is invalid.

    • 6
      Doing These Actions Consecutively (Muwālāh)

      Without delaying the washing of the next part so long that the part before it has dried. The actions must follow one after another without long pauses.

    💡 Amputated limbs: If part of the limb that is obligatory to wash is still there, then it should be washed; otherwise the requirement is waived. For artificial limbs that can easily be removed, they should be removed during wudu. If not possible, one should wipe over that part.
    💡 False teeth/dentures/dental implants: These do not have any impact on rinsing the mouth, and there is no need to take them out even if taking them out is easy — based on the hadith of Arfajah ibn As'ad who wore a false nose of gold by the Prophet's ﷺ instruction.

    Sunnahs of Wudu, Things that Invalidate It & Uncertainty

    This chapter covers the recommended (Sunnah) acts that perfect wudu, the 4 nullifiers, and what to do when uncertain about one's state of purity.

    ⭐

    The 10 Sunnah Acts of Wudu

    • 1
      Using the Siwāk

      Because the Prophet ﷺ said: «Were it not that it would be too difficult for my ummah, I would have instructed them to use the siwak every time they did wudu.» (Narrated by Ahmad; sahih by al-Albani)

    • 2
      Saying Bismillah

      Because of the hadith: «There is no wudu for the one who does not mention the name of Allah when doing it.» (Narrated by Abu Dawud; hasan by al-Albani)

    • 3
      Washing the Hands Three Times when Starting

      As Uthman described how the Prophet ﷺ did wudu: «He called for water, and poured water on his hands three times and washed them, then he put his hand in the vessel...» (Agreed upon)

    • 4
      Being Thorough in Taking Water into the Nose

      Because of the hadith: «Be thorough in taking water up into the nose, unless you are fasting.» (Narrated by Abu Dawud; sahih by al-Albani)

    • 5
      Running the Fingers Through a Thick Beard

      Because of the hadith of Anas: when the Messenger of Allah ﷺ did wudu, he would take a handful of water and put it under his jaw, running his fingers through his beard. (Narrated by Abu Dawud; sahih by al-Albani)

    • 6
      Taking Fresh Water to Wipe the Head

      In the hadith of Abdullah ibn Zayd: «And he wiped his head with fresh water, other than that which was already on his hand.» (Narrated by Muslim)

    • 7
      Rubbing Between the Fingers and Toes

      Because of the hadith of Ibn Abbas: «When you do wudu, rub between your fingers and toes.» (Narrated by at-Tirmidhi; sahih by al-Albani). If water cannot reach between them except by rubbing, then rubbing becomes obligatory.

    • 8
      Starting on the Right Before the Left

      When washing the hands and feet. Because of the hadith of Aisha: «The Prophet ﷺ liked to start on the right when putting on his sandals, combing his hair, purifying himself and in all his affairs.» (Agreed upon)

    • 9
      Washing the Face, Arms and Feet Three Times

      What is obligatory is to wash once; what is preferred is to wash three times. It is proven that the Prophet ﷺ washed each part once, and also twice, and it is proven that he washed his face three times and his arms twice.

    • 10
      Mentioning Allah (Dhikr) After Wudu

      Because of the hadith: «There is no one among you who does wudu and does it well, then says 'Ashhadu an laa ilaaha illa Allah wa anna Muhammadan abdullahi wa rasooluh', but the eight gates of Paradise will be opened for him, to enter through whichever of them he wishes.»

      Narrated by Muslim
    ✅ Drying the limbs after doing wudu is permissible, because the basic principle is that things are allowed, and there is nothing in the religious texts to disallow that.
    🚫

    Things that Invalidate Wudu (Nawāqiḍ) — 4 Nullifiers

    1. Anything that comes out of the front and back passages

    Whether it is urine, stools, wind, semen, madhiy, wadiy, or anything else, such as secretions. Allah says: «for one of you comes from the place of relieving himself» [al-Ma'idah 5:6]. The Prophet ﷺ said: «Allah does not accept the prayer of one of you if he invalidates his wudu until he does wudu again.» (Agreed upon)

    2. Loss of reason due to intoxication, fainting, sleep or insanity

    Loss of reason is more intense than sleep. The type of sleep that invalidates wudu is deep sleep in which a person is no longer conscious. Light sleep does NOT invalidate wudu — the Companions used to wait for Isha prayer, doze off, then pray without doing wudu again. (Abu Dawud; hasan by al-Albani)

    3. Touching the private part of a human being with no barrier

    Because of the hadith of Busrah bint Safwan: «Whoever touches his penis, let him do wudu.» (Narrated by the five; sahih by al-Albani). This applies to both men and women, front and back passages. Touching the testicles and buttocks does NOT invalidate wudu.

    4. Eating camel meat

    A man asked the Prophet ﷺ: Should I do wudu after eating mutton? He said: «If you wish, do wudu.» Should I do wudu after eating camel meat? He said: «Yes, do wudu after eating camel meat.» Can I pray in sheepfolds? «Yes.» Can I pray in camel pens? «No.» (Muslim)

    🤔

    Being Uncertain About One's State of Purity

    Case 1: Was in a state of purity, not sure if wudu was invalidated

    He is deemed to still be in a state of purity and no attention is to be paid to his uncertainty, because there is certainty about his being in a state of purity, and that cannot be changed except on the basis of certainty.

    Case 2: Is sure he invalidated his wudu, uncertain if he did wudu again

    He should proceed on the basis of what he is certain of — that he has invalidated his wudu, and no attention is to be paid to what he is uncertain about, because certainty about invalidation cannot be changed except by certainty of a new wudu.

    📿 Evidence — The Principle of Certainty

    «He should not stop praying unless he hears a sound or notices a smell.» (Agreed upon — if one feels something whilst praying but is unsure)

    Hadith of Abbad ibn Tamim from his paternal uncle
    📿 Evidence 2

    «If one of you feels something in his stomach, but he is not sure whether something came out of him or not, he should not leave the mosque unless he hears a sound or notices a smell.»

    Narrated by Muslim — Hadith of Abu Hurayrah

    Wiping over the Khuffs or Socks

    Islam provides the concession of wiping over the khuffs (leather socks) or regular socks instead of washing the feet in wudu, subject to specific conditions and time limits.

    🧦

    Definition & Evidence for Wiping over Khuffs

    📚 What is a Khuff?

    The khuff is a type of footwear made of thin leather and the like, which covers the ankles and more — like socks.

    Evidence for Prescription:

    📖 The verse: «and wipe over your heads and wash your feet to the ankles» [al-Ma'idah 5:6] — if "feet" is read in the genitive case, the meaning is: wipe over your heads and [over] your feet to the ankles.
    📿 There are mutawatir hadiths from the Prophet ﷺ to that effect — approximately forty hadiths concerning wiping over the khuffs.
    📋

    Four Conditions for Wiping over Khuffs

    • 1
      They should have been put on when one was in a state of purity

      Because the Prophet ﷺ said to al-Mugheerah ibn Shu'bah: «Leave them — meaning the khuffs — because I have put them on in a state of purity.» (Narrated by al-Bukhari and Muslim)

    • 2
      The khuffs should be pure (tahir)

      If contaminated with impurities, it is not permissible to wipe over them. Evidence: The Messenger of Allah ﷺ led his Companions in prayer wearing sandals, then he took them off during the prayer, and he told the people that Jibreel had informed him there was something dirty on them. (Narrated by ad-Darima with a sahih isnad)

    • 3
      Wiping can only be done for minor impurity, not major impurity

      Because Safwan ibn Assal said: The Messenger of Allah ﷺ instructed us not to take off our khuffs for three days and nights, except in the case of janabah (major impurity), so we could keep them on in the event of defecation, urination and sleep. (Narrated by Ahmad and at-Tirmidhi; sahih)

    • 4
      Wiping should be done within the stipulated time limit

      Because of the hadith of Ali ibn Abi Talib: «The Prophet ﷺ set a time limit of three days and three nights for one who is travelling, and one day and one night for one who is not travelling, meaning for wiping over the khuffs.» (Narrated by Muslim)

    ⏱️

    Time Limits for Wiping over Khuffs

    Non-traveller

    One day and one night (24 hours). Begins from the first time the individual wipes over his khuffs after invalidating his wudu, and ends twenty-four hours later.

    Traveller

    Three days and three nights (72 hours). Begins from the first time wiping after invalidation of wudu during travel.

    💡 Example: Someone does wudu for Fajr on Tuesday, then puts on his socks and remains in a state of purity until he prays Isha on Tuesday night. Then he gets up on Wednesday and wipes over his socks at 5am. The period begins at 5am Wednesday and lasts until 5am Thursday.
    💡 Important: If the time limit ends, he may NOT wipe over them again — but his current wudu remains valid. Because the time limit stipulates when wiping may be done, not when the wudu becomes invalid. His wudu only becomes invalid when a nullifier occurs.
    🔍

    Things that May be Wiped Over

    • 1
      Socks and boots that cover the ankles

      Evidence: The hadith of al-Mugheerah ibn Shu'bah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ did wudu and wiped over his socks and sandals. (Narrated by Ahmad and at-Tirmidhi; sahih). Ibn al-Mundhir said this is narrated from nine of the Companions.

      ✅ Wiping over socks may be done without any conditions stipulated for that, according to the correct scholarly view, so long as they are still fit to be called socks. It does not matter if they are torn or have holes.
    • 2
      Turbans

      Evidence: The hadith of Thawban that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ sent out an expedition and instructed them to wipe over their turbans and khuffs. (Narrated by Ahmad, Abu Dawud, and al-Hakim; sahih). Also: Amr ibn Umayyah said he saw the Messenger of Allah ﷺ wiping over his turban and khuffs. (Narrated by al-Bukhari and Muslim)

    • 3
      Women's Headcovers

      A woman may wipe over her headcover if she needs to because of cold weather, sickness and the like, or if it is difficult to take it off. Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah said: If a woman is afraid of the cold, she may wipe over her head cover, because Umm Salamah used to wipe over her head cover.

    • 4
      Plaster Casts and Surgical Dressings

      Evidence: The hadith of Jabir — a man was hit by a stone causing a head injury, then he had a wet dream. He asked his companions about tayammum. They said: We do not know of any concession. So he did ghusl and died. The Prophet ﷺ said: «They have killed him, may Allah kill them! Why did they not ask if they did not know? The one who is ignorant can solve his problem by asking. It would have sufficed him to do tayammum, then to cover his wound with a piece of cloth and wipe over it, then wash the rest of his body.» (Narrated by Abu Dawud and Ibn Majah; sahih by al-Albani)

    Differences: Plaster Casts vs. Khuffs

    Plaster casts — Wipe the ENTIRE cast

    Not just the top of it.

    Khuffs — Wipe only the TOP

    Only the top of the khuffs or socks should be wiped, not the bottom. This is what is proven in the Sunnah.

    Plaster cast — No time limit

    Until the individual is healed or takes it off. Differs from khuffs which have a stipulated time limit.

    Plaster cast — Valid for both major and minor impurity

    Unlike khuffs which is only for minor impurity, plaster casts may be wiped when purifying from both minor and major impurity.

    💡 Where to wipe: Only the top of the khuffs or socks (not the bottom) · The entire plaster cast or surgical dressing · Only the location of the head in the case of head covers and turbans.
    📚

    Miscellaneous Issues — Wiping over Khuffs

    • If a traveller was wiping over his khuffs, then he reached his place of residence, he may continue to wipe over them as a non-traveller (one day and one night).
    • A non-traveller wiped over his khuffs, then he started his journey — he may continue to wipe over his khuffs as a non-traveller (one day and one night).
    • A traveller is not sure whether he started to wipe when he was not travelling or after he started his journey — he may continue to wipe as a non-traveller (the safer option).
    • A non-traveller invalidated his wudu, then did wudu and began to wipe over his khuffs after he started his journey — he may continue to wipe as a traveller (three days and three nights).
    💡 Ruling on wiping over wigs: It is NOT permissible to wear a wig, because it comes under the same heading as hair extensions, which are prohibited. Exception: a bald woman with no hair. If taking it off is difficult (except at a doctor's office), she may wipe over it without removing it.

    Ghusl — The Ritual Bath

    Ghusl is the full-body ritual purification that removes major impurity (ḥadath akbar). Understanding when it is obligatory and how to perform it correctly is essential.

    📖

    Definition of Ghusl

    Linguistic Meaning

    Letting water flow over the body whilst rubbing the skin.

    Islamic Terminology

    Ghusl means pouring pure water over the entire body with the intention of removing impurity.

    ⚠️

    Things That Make Ghusl Obligatory (5 Causes)

    • 1
      Emission of Maniy (Semen) — With Pleasure

      Emission of semen forcefully, with feelings of pleasure, as a result of intercourse or a wet dream makes ghusl obligatory.

      📿 Evidence

      «Water for water.» (Narrated by Muslim) — meaning ghusl is required when semen is emitted. Also: Umm Sulaym asked the Prophet ﷺ: Does a woman have to do ghusl if she has a wet dream? He said: «Yes, if she sees a discharge.» (Agreed upon)

    • 2
      Penetration — Even Without Ejaculation

      Penetration of the penis into the vagina makes ghusl obligatory, even if no ejaculation occurs.

      📿 Evidence

      «When a man sits between the four parts (arms and legs of his wife) and the one circumcised part touches the other, then ghusl becomes obligatory.» According to another version: «even if no ejaculation occurs.» (Narrated by Muslim)

    • 3
      Menses (Hayd)

      When a woman's menstrual period ends, ghusl becomes obligatory. The woman who does ghusl following menses should rub her head thoroughly.

      📿 Evidence

      The hadith of Aisha concerning the case of Fatimah bint Abi Hubaysh: «That is a vein and is not menses, so when your regular time of menses comes, stop praying, and when it ends, wash the blood from you, then pray.» (Agreed upon). Also: The Messenger of Allah ﷺ instructed Umm Habibah to do ghusl. (Agreed upon)

      ⚖️ When to Do Ghusl

      Ghusl is done after the end of menses. If she does ghusl before her menses ends, her ghusl is not valid, because Allah says: «And do not approach them until they are pure. And when they have purified themselves, then come to them» [al-Baqarah 2:222].

    • 4
      Nifas (Post-Childbirth Bleeding)

      Nifas is bleeding that occurs at or following childbirth, or two or three days before it accompanied by contractions. Scholars are unanimously agreed that ghusl after nifas is as obligatory as it is after menses.

      💡 Bleeding that may occur during pregnancy or at the end of pregnancy without contractions does not come under the heading of nifas.
    • 5
      Death

      When a Muslim dies, it is obligatory for the living to wash the deceased, because the deceased is no longer accountable after death.

      📿 Evidence

      The hadith of Umm Atiyah: The Messenger of Allah ﷺ came in whilst they were washing his daughter and said: «Wash her three times, or five, or more than that, with water and lotus leaves, and put some camphor in the water the last time.» (Agreed upon)

      💡 Ruling on washing a miscarried foetus: If the soul has been breathed into the foetus (after 4 months), it should be washed. If not yet breathed into it, it is not obligatory to wash it.
    ✨

    Types of Ghusl That Are Encouraged (Mustahabb)

    • When a disbeliever becomes Muslim — The Prophet ﷺ instructed new converts to do ghusl with water and lotus leaves.
    • After washing a deceased person — «Whoever has washed a deceased person, let him do ghusl, and whoever has carried the deceased, let him do wudu.» (Ahmad and at-Tirmidhi; hasan)
    • Recovering consciousness after having fainted — Because of the hadith of Aisha: the Prophet ﷺ asked for water to be put in a tub for him (twice) and did ghusl. (Agreed upon)
    • Recovering from insanity — By analogy with recovering from fainting.
    • Every seven days (Friday ghusl) — «It is the duty of every Muslim to do ghusl one day in every seven days, in which he washes his head and his body.» (Agreed upon)
    • For the two Eids — Ghusl is encouraged before both Eid prayers.
    • For the woman suffering from istihadah — She should do ghusl for every prayer.
    • Before entering ihram for Hajj or Umrah.
    📋

    Description of Ghusl — 6 Steps

    📿 Based on the Report in as-Sahihayn

    When the Prophet ﷺ did ghusl to purify himself from janabah, he started by washing his hands, then he poured water with his right hand into his left hand and washed his private part, then he did wudu as for prayer, then he took water and used his fingers to make it reach the roots of his hair, then he poured water over his head three times, then he poured water over the rest of his body, then he washed his feet.

    • 1
      Forming the Intention (Niyyah) and Saying Bismillah

      The intention is an act of the heart, not something that needs to be uttered aloud.

    • 2
      Washing the Hands Three Times

      Washing both hands thoroughly at the start.

    • 3
      Washing the Private Part

      Using the left hand to wash away any impurity from the private area.

    • 4
      Doing Wudu as for Prayer

      Performing complete wudu (rinsing mouth, nose, washing face, arms, wiping head, washing feet).

    • 5
      Pouring Water Over the Head Three Times

      Making sure the water reaches every part of the hair, including the roots, by running the fingers through. According to another report: «then when he was certain that he had made the water reach the scalp, he poured water over his head three times.»

    • 6
      Pouring Water Over the Entire Body

      Making sure the water reaches every part of the body, starting on the right side and then the left. The feet are washed last (or already done in wudu).

    ⚖️ Minimum Valid Ghusl

    The minimum that is acceptable in ghusl is to pour water over the entire body, because the Prophet ﷺ said to the man who was junub and had not prayed: «Take this and pour it over yourself.» (Agreed upon)

    Tayammum — Dry Purification

    Tayammum is an alternative to wudu and ghusl when water cannot be found or used. It is a unique blessing given to this ummah.

    📖

    Definition of Tayammum

    Linguistic Meaning

    To aim towards something. It appears in the verse: «And do not aim toward [laa tayammamu] the defective therefrom, spending [from that]» [al-Baqarah 2:267].

    Islamic Terminology

    Tayammum refers to wiping the face and hands with clean earth, in a specific manner, as an act of devotion and worship of Allah ﷻ.

    📿 A Unique Gift to This Ummah

    «I have been given five things that were not given to any of the prophets before me: I have been supported with fear [cast into the hearts of my enemies] from a distance of one month, and the earth has been made a place of prostration and a means of purification for me…»

    Agreed upon — Jabir ibn Abd Allah
    💡 Tayammum is an alternative to wudu and ghusl when it is not possible to do them. It removes hadath (the state of impurity) temporarily, until one is able to find water.
    ❓

    When Is Tayammum Prescribed?

    ⚖️ Core Rule

    Tayammum is prescribed when one is not able to use water — either because he cannot find it, or is not capable of using it.

    Not Able to Find Water

    When someone is in the desert with no water, detained in a room with no water, or water is too far away according to what is customarily regarded as too far away.

    Not Capable of Using Water

    Water is available but the individual cannot use it — for fear of physical harm, because he needs it to drink, or because it is extremely cold.

    📿 Evidence

    «Indeed, clean earth is a means of purification for the Muslim, even if he cannot find water for ten years, then if he finds water, let him make it touch his skin.»

    Narrated by at-Tirmidhi; classed as sahih by al-Albani
    💡 The verse [an-Nisa 4:43] indicates it is not permissible to do tayammum except after trying to find water. Thus tayammum is a concession for the one who cannot find water — and in Arabic it cannot be said that "he did not find water" except in the case of one who looked for it and did not find what he wanted.
    ✅ Tayammum is also prescribed in cases where it is encouraged (mustahabb) to purify oneself. The Prophet ﷺ did tayammum in order to return the greeting of salam, saying: «I did not like to mention Allah except in a state of purity.» (Narrated by Abu Dawud; classed as sahih by al-Albani)
    ✅ It is permissible to do tayammum before the time for prayer begins. The individual may then pray with that tayammum whatever obligatory and supererogatory prayers he wishes. But once water becomes available, tayammum ceases to be valid.
    🤚

    With What Can One Do Tayammum?

    • Pure dust or earth that has particles which cling to the hand — based on the Quranic verse [al-Ma'idah 5:6].
    • If he cannot find dust or earth, he may do tayammum with sand or rocks, because Allah says: «So fear Allah as much as you are able» [at-Taghabun 64:16].
    • It is essential that the dust or earth be pure (tahir) — it is not valid to do tayammum with impure dust or earth contaminated with urine, because Allah says: «then seek clean earth» [an-Nisa 4:43].
    📿 Evidence

    «The entire earth has been made a place of prostration for us, and its dust (or soil) has been made a means of purification for us.»

    Narrated by Muslim — Hudhayfah
    📋

    Obligatory Parts of Tayammum — 4 Acts

    1. Forming the Intention (Niyyah)

    An act of worship in the heart. The intention is to remove hadath and make worship permissible.

    2. Wiping the Face

    Wiping the entire face once using both hands after striking the ground.

    3. Wiping the Hands — Right First, Then Left

    Because of the verse: «and wipe over your faces and your hands with it» [an-Nisa 4:43]. Ammar ibn Yasir said the Prophet ﷺ struck the ground with his hands, then wiped his face and hands.

    4. Doing These Actions in Order

    Face before hands — because of the verse: «and wipe over your faces and your hands with it» [an-Nisa 4:43].

    💡 If one fears the time for prayer will end if he uses water: What is obligatory is still to do wudu or ghusl, even if one fears the time for prayer will end. The majority of scholars hold that the individual must do ghusl, because he has an excuse, and it is not prescribed for him to do tayammum in that case.
    🔄

    Description of Tayammum — 3 Steps

    • 1
      Strike the Ground Once

      Strike the ground with your hands once. The scholars regard it as preferred (mustahabb) to hold the fingers apart when doing so.

    • 2
      Wipe the Face

      Wipe the entire face with both hands.

    • 3
      Wipe the Hands

      Wipe the back of one hand with the palm of the other, wiping the right first, then the left.

    ❌

    Things That Invalidate Tayammum

    1. Everything That Invalidates Wudu

    Because tayammum is an alternative to wudu, it comes under the same rulings.

    2. Water Becoming Available When One Is Able to Use It

    Once water becomes accessible and the person is capable of using it, tayammum ceases to be valid.

    💧

    Finding Water: Three Scenarios

    • 1
      Finds Water Before Starting to Pray

      He must do wudu. It is not permissible for him to pray with tayammum.

    • 2
      Finds Water After He Has Prayed

      There is no blame on him and he does not have to repeat the prayer. Based on the story narrated by Abu Dawud of two men who did tayammum and prayed, then found water before the time for prayer ended. One repeated the prayer; the other did not. The Prophet ﷺ said to the one who did not repeat: «You acted in accordance with the Sunnah.» And to the one who repeated: «You will have a twofold reward.» (Classed as sahih by al-Albani)

    • 3
      Finds Water Whilst He Is Praying

      This invalidates tayammum, because Allah says: «and do not find water» [al-Ma'idah 5:6]. He should exit the prayer, do wudu, and start his prayer all over again. When water does become available, the alternative is no longer valid, so the ruling no longer applies.

    💡 If there is no water and no dust or earth (e.g., someone detained where there is neither), he should pray as he is, so as to offer the prayer on time — which is the most important condition of prayer. He does not have to repeat it later. This is described as "lacking the two means of purification."

    Removing Physical Impurities (Najasah)

    Najasah refers to every dirty substance that the Lawgiver has instructed Muslims to avoid. This chapter covers the categories, rulings, and methods of removing physical impurities.

    📖

    Definition & Categories of Najasah

    📚 Definition

    Physical impurity (najasah) refers to every dirty substance that the Lawgiver has instructed to be avoided.

    1. Accidental Impurity

    Something that is pure (tahir) becomes contaminated with an impure substance — e.g., a garment that is pure, then some impurity falls onto it. Ruling: It may be purified by any means of removing the impure substance.

    2. Inherent Impurity

    A thing that is impure (najis) in and of itself — such as dogs, pigs, donkey droppings, menstrual blood, urine, stools. Ruling: This impurity cannot be purified — if a dog were washed with all the water of the sea, it would never become pure.

    🧹

    How to Purify a Contaminated Place

    Category 1 — Contamination with the Impurity of a Dog

    The place must be washed seven times, the first of which should be with dust or earth.

    📿 Evidence

    «The way to purify the vessel of one of you, if a dog licks it, is to wash it seven times, the first of which should be with dust or earth.»

    Narrated by Muslim
    💡 No differentiation is made between black dogs and dogs of other colours, trained and untrained dogs, young and old dogs, or those it is permissible or not permissible to keep. All types of dogs come under this ruling. Pigs are not treated in the same manner as dogs in terms of this seven-time washing requirement.
    🔬 Scientific note: Viruses carried by dogs are extremely small and able to cling to vessel walls. Dog saliva contains viruses that resemble a string of saliva. Dust/earth absorbs these viruses. It has been scientifically proven that dust contains tetracycline and tetralite — both used in sterilization. However, Muslims adhere to the command of the Lawgiver regardless of these physical benefits.

    Category 2 — Contamination with Other Impurities

    It is sufficient to wash the place once to remove the impure substance. If not removed by washing once, wash again until it is removed.

    ⚖️ Important Rule

    If the impure substance has physical mass (e.g., stools), it must be removed first, then that spot should be washed with water.

    📿 Evidence — Bedouin Who Urinated in the Mosque

    A Bedouin came and urinated in some spot in the mosque. The people rebuked him, but the Messenger of Allah ﷺ told them not to rebuke him. When he finished urinating, the Prophet ﷺ instructed that a bucket of water be brought and poured over it.

    Agreed upon — Hadith of Anas
    📊

    Three Degrees of Impurity (Najasah)

    Light (Mukhaffafah)

    Such as the urine of an infant boy who is not yet eating solid food. Purified by sprinkling water on it without rubbing or squeezing the fabric, until the water has covered the entire area.

    Moderate (Mutawassitah)

    Such as the urine and stools of human beings; the blood of menses and nifas; and most other impurities. Purified by washing until the impure substance is removed.

    Severe (Mughallaẓah)

    The impurity (najasah) of dogs and anything that they lick. Must be washed seven times, the first of which is with dust or earth.

    💡 Baby girl's urine: In the case of a baby girl, her urine must be washed off in all cases — it does not have the concession of sprinkling water. Only a baby boy has this concession.
    ⚖️ Is Water a Condition for Purification?

    Purification is attained by any means that removes the physical impurity, whether that is done with water, sunshine, fresh air, or the passage of time. Once the impurity has been removed, the ruling no longer applies. This includes dry-cleaning and steam cleaning — if the impurity has been removed, the item is no longer deemed impure.

    ✅

    Pure Things That Are Sometimes Disputed

    • The believer does not become impure (najis) when he dies — Based on the general meaning of: «The believer does not become impure (najis).» (Agreed upon)
    • That which does not have flowing blood — Insects such as flies, scorpions and the like are pure, provided they were not born in a place of impurity. Evidence: «If a fly falls into the drink of one of you, let him immerse it fully, then throw it away.» (al-Bukhari)
    • Urine, dung and semen of animals whose meat may be eaten — Such as camels, sheep, cattle, chickens. Evidence: The hadith about some people who were told to drink camel urine and milk (Agreed upon), and: «Pray in sheepfolds» (as-Sahihayn).
    • Human semen — Aisha رضي الله عنها said: I used to scratch off semen from the garment of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, then he would go and pray wearing it. (Narrated by Muslim)
    • Moisture from a woman's vagina — There is no report from the Prophet ﷺ to suggest that he gave instructions that it be washed off.
    • Leftover food and water of cats and other animals that come and go in houses — because there is no religious text stating they are impure. Evidence: «They are not impure; they are among those who go around among you.» (Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi; sahih by al-Albani)
    🩸

    Types of Blood — Purity Rulings

    Human Blood:

    Blood of Menses and Nifas

    Impure (najis) according to scholarly consensus. Evidence: A woman asked the Prophet ﷺ about menstrual blood on her garment. He said: «Scratch it, then rub it with water, then rinse it, then she may pray wearing it.» (Agreed upon)

    Blood Other Than Menses and Nifas

    Such as blood from the body, teeth, wounds. This is pure according to the most correct scholarly opinion. Evidence: It is not known that the Prophet ﷺ instructed people to wash off any blood except menstrual blood. Muslims have always prayed with wounds, and there is no report to suggest washing off that blood.

    Blood of Animals:

    Blood of Animals Whose Meat May Be Eaten

    Pure (tahir) — there is no evidence to indicate it is impure. Exception: blood that is "spilled out" (drained at slaughter) is excluded by Quranic verse [al-An'am 6:145]. Blood remaining in veins, heart, spleen and liver after proper slaughter is pure.

    Blood of Animals Whose Meat Cannot Be Eaten / Not Slaughtered Properly

    Impure (najis), except in the case of cats and animals that come and go in houses. Also: blood of animals that died without being slaughtered in the prescribed manner is impure according to scholarly consensus. Exception: fish and locusts.

    📿 Evidence — Dead Meat Exception

    «Two types of 'dead meat' and two types of blood have been permitted for us. As for the two types of 'dead meat', they are fish and locusts. As for the two types of blood, they are the liver and the spleen.»

    Narrated by Ahmad and Ibn Majah; classed as sahih by al-Albani
    🔍

    Special Rulings — Madhiy, Wadiy & Unknown Location of Impurity

    Madhiy

    A thin liquid emitted when feeling desire. It is impure (najis) according to scholarly consensus. Purified by washing the penis and testicles, then sprinkling water on the garment.

    Wadiy

    A thick, white liquid emitted immediately after urinating. Requires washing oneself with water (istinja) and purifying clothes and body of it. It is impure (najis) and necessitates wudu according to scholarly consensus.

    When the Location of Impurity Is Unknown — 4 Scenarios:

    • You are certain the impure substance got on both spots → wash both.
    • You are certain it got on one specific spot → wash that one only.
    • You think it most likely that it got on one of the two → wash that one only.
    • You think it is equally possible it could be on either spot → wash both.
    💡 The stools of an infant boy or girl come under the same rulings as other impure substances — it is obligatory to wash that place, and it is not sufficient to sprinkle it with water.

    Menses (Hayd) & Nifas

    This chapter covers the rulings on menses (hayd), post-childbirth bleeding (nifas), irregular bleeding (istihadah), and the practical rulings that govern these states.

    📖

    Definition of Hayd (Menses)

    Linguistic Meaning

    Hayd means flow, so one may say "haada al-wadi (the valley flowed with water)" if water flows through it.

    Islamic Teaching

    Hayd refers to the natural blood that emerges from deep inside the uterus at certain times, when a woman is in good health, and it is not connected to childbirth.

    «And they ask you about menstruation. Say, "It is harm"»
    [al-Baqarah 2:222]
    ⏱️

    Duration & Age of Menses

    Age When Menses Begins

    A female cannot menstruate before the age of nine years, because there is no proof that any woman ever began to menstruate before that age. Women do not menstruate after reaching the age of fifty years, in most cases.

    Duration of Menses

    Minimum: One day and one night.
    Maximum: Fifteen days (according to most fuqaha). Anything more than that is istihadah.
    Usual: Six or seven days.

    ⚠️ If a woman sees blood but it does not continue for one day and one night, then it is irregular bleeding and does not come under the rulings on menses — yet it is still to be regarded as impure (najis) and invalidates wudu.
    ✅

    When Does Menses End?

    The end of menses occurs when one of two things happens:

    1. Cessation of Bleeding

    A woman may recognize this by inserting a piece of cloth or cotton into her vagina; if it comes out clean, with no brownish or yellowish discharge on it, then this is a sign that her period has ended.

    2. Emission of White Discharge

    The white discharge (qassah baydaa) is a clear liquid excreted by the uterus when menses ends. Evidence: Aisha رضي الله عنها said: «Do not hasten until you see the white discharge — meaning the sign that menses had ended.» (Narrated by Malik; classed as sahih by al-Albani)

    Brownish/Yellowish Discharge Connected to Menses

    Coming before or after menses, part of menses.

    Brownish/Yellowish Discharge Not Connected to Menses

    If it comes before menses then stops, or comes after menses has ended, it does not come under the heading of menses. Umm Atiyah رضي الله عنها said: «We used to regard the yellowish or brownish discharge after the tuhr (end of menses) as not being of any significance.» (al-Bukhari)

    ⚖️ First-Time Menstruation

    In the case of bleeding for a female who has never menstruated before, if the blood has the same characteristics as menstrual blood, then this is menses. But if it lasts for more than fifteen days, anything after fifteen days is istihadah.

    🚫

    Things Prohibited During Menses and Nifas — 7 Prohibitions

    • 1
      Prayer

      The Prophet ﷺ said to Fatimah bint Abi Hubaysh: «When your regular time of menses comes, stop praying.» (Agreed upon). She does not make up missed prayers.

    • 2
      Fasting

      «Is it not the case that when one of you menstruates, she does not fast and she does not pray?» (Agreed upon). She must make up missed fasts later.

    • 3
      Tawaf

      The Prophet ﷺ said to Aisha when she menstruated: «Do everything that the pilgrim does, except do not circumambulate the Kaaba [tawaf] until your menses ends.» (Agreed upon)

    • 4
      Touching the Mushaf (Quran)

      «No one should touch the Mushaf except one who is in a state of purity.» (Narrated by Malik; classed as sahih by al-Albani). However, it is permissible to touch it with a barrier.

      💡 Can a menstruating woman recite Quran? The more correct scholarly view is that it is permissible for her to recite Quran, especially if she needs to do so (as a student or teacher). She may recite her hizb or more, without touching the Mushaf — as there is no clear evidence in the religious texts to indicate that it is not allowed.
    • 5
      Entering the Mosque and Staying There

      Similarly, it is haram for a menstruating woman to pass through the mosque if she fears she may contaminate it with her blood. If there is no risk of contamination, then it is not forbidden.

    • 6
      Intercourse in the Vagina

      Allah says: «So keep away from women during menstruation. And do not approach them until they are pure» [al-Baqarah 2:222]. The Prophet ﷺ said: «Do everything except intercourse.» (Narrated by Muslim)

    • 7
      Divorce

      Allah says: «O Prophet, when you [Muslims] divorce women, divorce them for [the commencement of] their waiting period» [at-Talaq 65:1]. The Prophet ﷺ said to Umar when his son divorced his wife during her menses: «Tell him to take her back, then divorce her when she is in a state of purity [not menstruating] or pregnant.» (Narrated by Muslim)

    🩺

    Istihadah — Irregular Non-Menstrual Bleeding

    📚 Definition

    Istihadah is the blood a woman sees following her menses that has different characteristics to menstrual blood. It differs from menstrual blood in terms of rulings and characteristics.

    ⚖️ How Should the Woman Who Suffers From Istihadah Pray?

    She should wash her private part, then put on a pad, and do wudu for each prayer after the time for that prayer begins. She may then offer both obligatory and supererogatory prayers.

    Three Scenarios for the Woman with Istihadah:

    • 1
      She Has a Known, Regular Cycle

      She should stop praying at the time of her regular menses only, then resume after menses ends.

    • 2
      No Regular Cycle But Can Tell the Difference Between Bleeding Types

      She acts on the basis of distinguishing between types of bleeding. Her menses is when the bleeding has distinct characteristics (dark, thick, or has a certain odour). Everything other than that is istihadah.

    • 3
      No Regular Cycle and No Change in Bleeding Characteristics

      She should follow the usual cycle of the majority of women — menses lasts six or seven days every month. Everything apart from that is istihadah.

    ✅ The woman who suffers from istihadah comes under the same rulings as women who are in a state of purity in all matters — her fasting, prayer and tawaf are valid; it is permissible for her to recite Quran and touch the Mushaf; and it is permissible for her to have intercourse with her husband. She must only do wudu for each prayer after the time for it begins.
    👶

    Nifas — Post-Childbirth Bleeding

    📚 Definition

    Nifas (lochia) is the bleeding that comes from a woman's uterus due to giving birth. It is the remaining blood that was detained in the uterus during the pregnancy. In most cases it has the characteristics of menstrual blood.

    Duration of Nifas

    Minimum: No minimum duration — may last several days.
    Maximum: Forty days. Anything more than forty days is istihadah.

    Rulings of Nifas

    Nifas is like menses with regard to what is permissible, what is prohibited, and what is haram — intimacy without intercourse, fasting, prayer, divorce, tawaf, and remaining in the mosque. Nifas is like menses in all rulings.

    ⚖️ After Nifas Ends

    The woman is required to do ghusl when the bleeding of nifas ends. She must make up any fasts missed, but not prayers. If the bleeding of nifas stops before forty days, then the woman's nifas has ended — she must do ghusl and pray, and the things that were not allowed now become permissible for her.

    💡 Miscarriage and nifas: If a miscarriage occurs and there are clear rudimentary human features in the foetus, then the rulings of nifas apply. The stage at which human features appear is usually around three months; the minimum is eighty-one days. If the embryo is at the alaqah (clot of blood) or mudghah (lump of flesh) stage with no human features, the bleeding after that is not nifas — the woman should not stop praying and fasting.
    🔍

    The Four Categories of Bleeding Women May Experience

    State of Purity (Tuhr)

    One who is not menstruating and not bleeding at all.

    Menstrual Bleeding (Hayd)

    One who sees menstrual blood at the usual time of menses, that matches the stipulated description.

    Istihadah

    One who sees blood following her menses, that is different in nature to menstrual blood.

    Irregular Bleeding

    One who sees blood that is not connected to her menses. Any bleeding a woman sees at a time other than her menses, or not connected to her menses. Does not prevent prayer and fasting, but is impure (najis) and invalidates wudu.

    ⚖️ Related Issues — Menses and Prayer/Fasting

    If a woman gets her menses after the time for a prayer has begun and she has not yet offered that prayer, she must make it up after her menses ends. If a woman gets her menses on a day when she is fasting, her fast becomes invalid and she must make it up. If menses ends on the day of a fast, she must make up that day, but does not have to refrain from eating and drinking for the rest of that day.

    Prayer (Salah) — Importance, Virtue & Rulings

    Prayer is the second pillar of Islam and the most important act of worship after the two declarations of faith. This chapter covers its definition, importance, virtue, and who is obliged to pray.

    📖

    Definition of Prayer (Salah)

    Linguistic Meaning

    The word salat (prayer) means supplication or invocation. Allah ﷻ says: «And invoke [Allah's blessings] upon them [wa salli 'alayhim]. Indeed, your invocations are reassurance for them» [at-Tawbah 9:103]. In other words, offer supplication for them.

    Islamic Terminology

    The word salat (prayer) means worshipping Allah ﷻ with specific words and movements, starting with the takbeer and ending with the tasleem.

    🏛️

    Status of Prayer Within Islam

    Prayer is the second of the pillars of Islam after the twin declaration of faith (ash-shahadatayn).

    📿 The Five Pillars — Agreed Upon

    «Islam is built on five [pillars]: testifying that there is no god worthy of worship except Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, establishing prayer, giving zakat, Hajj, and fasting Ramadan.»

    Agreed upon
    «Indeed, prayer has been decreed upon the believers a decree of specified times»
    [an-Nisa 4:103]
    📿 Five Prayers Every Day

    The Prophet ﷺ sent Muadh رضي الله عنه to Yemen and said to him: «Inform them that Allah has enjoined upon them five prayers every day and night.»

    Agreed upon
    ⭐

    Why Is Prayer of Such Great Importance?

    • Because Allah ﷻ delayed making it obligatory until the night of the Isra and Miraj (Prophet's Night Journey and Ascent to Heaven), by way of emphasizing how important it is.
    • Because it was enjoined directly by Allah ﷻ upon His Messenger ﷺ without any intermediary.
    • For it was enjoined in heaven, which is indicative of its sublime status.
    • Originally, fifty prayers were enjoined, which is indicative of how much Allah loves it.
    💡 Prayer was enjoined on the night of the Isra and Miraj, before the Hijrah.
    ✨

    The Virtue of Prayer

    There are many hadiths which speak of the virtue of prayer and enjoin and exhort us to do it.

    📿 Hadith 1 — Expiation for Sins

    «The five daily prayers, one Jumuah to the next, and one Ramadan to the next, are an expiation for what comes between them, if one avoids major sins.»

    Narrated by Muslim — Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه
    📿 Hadith 2 — The River Analogy

    «What do you think if there was a river by the door of any one of you and he bathed in it five times a day, would there be any trace of dirt left on him?» They said, "No trace of dirt would be left on him." He said, «That is like the five daily prayers, by means of which Allah erases sin.»

    Agreed upon
    💡 The Wisdom Behind Prayer

    Prayer is gratitude for the immense blessings that Allah bestows upon His slaves. It is also one of the acts that show the highest level of servitude to Allah, in which the individual demonstrates his devotion to Allah ﷻ, humbles himself before Him and submits to Him, and converses with Him by reciting Quran, dhikr and dua. It is also the connection between a person and his Lord, which elevates him to a level in which he finds peace of mind and tranquillity.

    ⚖️

    Rulings on Prayer — Who Must Pray

    ⚖️ General Ruling

    The five daily prayers are obligatory for every adult Muslim of sound mind, whether male or female.

    Prayer Is NOT Obligatory For:

    • The insane — because reason is the basis of accountability.
    • Minors — for the same reason.
    • Women who are menstruating or bleeding following childbirth — based on: «Is it not the case that when she menstruates, she does not pray or fast?» (al-Bukhari and Muslim)

    Prayer of a Minor (Child)

    The child is to be instructed to pray at age 7, and may be lightly disciplined if he does not pray when he reaches the age of ten years. Evidence: «Instruct your children to pray when they reach the age of seven years, and smack them (lightly) if they do not pray when they reach the age of ten years, and separate them in their beds.» (Abu Dawud; sahih by al-Albani)

    Special Cases — Catching the Prayer Before It Ends:

    • If a child reaches puberty, a disbeliever becomes Muslim, or a woman who is menstruating becomes pure before the time for prayer ends by the length of time it takes to pray one rakah — they must offer that prayer. Evidence: «Whoever catches up with one rakah of the prayer has caught up with the prayer.» (Agreed upon)
    • The disbeliever is not to be instructed to make up the prayers missed before becoming Muslim, because Islam erases that which came before it, and the Prophet ﷺ did not instruct anyone who became Muslim to make up prayers.
    🚨

    Ruling on One Who Does Not Pray

    One Who Denies Prayer Is Obligatory

    He is a disbeliever according to scholarly consensus, because he has rejected the command of Allah and His Messenger ﷺ and the consensus of the ummah.

    One Who Neglects Prayer Out of Heedlessness or Laziness

    Whilst still affirming it is obligatory — there is a difference of scholarly opinion. The most likely correct view is that he is a disbeliever. Evidence from three texts:

    📿 Evidence 1 — Quranic Verse

    «But if they repent, establish prayer, and give zakah, then they are your brothers in religion» [at-Tawbah 9:11] — this indicates that establishing prayer is a condition for being a brother in religion.

    📿 Evidence 2 — Hadith of Jabir

    «Between a man and shirk and disbelief there stands his giving up prayer.»

    Narrated by Muslim
    📿 Evidence 3 — Hadith of Buraydah

    «The covenant between us and them [the hypocrites] is prayer; whoever gives it up has disbelieved.»

    Narrated by an-Nasai; classed as sahih by al-Albani

    Adhan and Iqamah

    The adhan is the call to prayer and the iqamah is the announcement that the prayer is about to begin. Both were prescribed in the first year after the Hijrah (1 AH).

    📖

    Definitions

    Adhan — Linguistic Meaning

    The word adhan means announcement. Allah ﷻ says: «And [it is] an announcement [adhaanun] from Allah and His Messenger to the people» [at-Tawbah 9:3].

    Adhan — Islamic Terminology

    An announcement, consisting of specific words, that the time for prayer has begun.

    📚 The Iqamah

    The iqamah is an announcement, consisting of specific words, that the prayer is about to begin.

    📜

    Prescription of the Adhan — How It Was Revealed

    The adhan was prescribed in the first year after the Hijrah (1 AH). The story is mentioned in the report narrated from Abdullah ibn Zayd رضي الله عنه:

    📿 The Dream of Abdullah ibn Zayd — Abu Dawud & at-Tirmidhi; sahih by al-Albani

    When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ instructed that a clapper be made to summon the people to prayer, Abdullah ibn Zayd saw in his dream a man carrying a clapper. He said: "O slave of Allah, will you sell me the clapper?" The man said: "What will you do with it?" He said: "We will call the people to prayer." The man said: "Shall I not tell you of something better than that?" and then taught him the words of the adhan. The following morning, Abdullah went to the Prophet ﷺ and told him what he had seen. The Prophet ﷺ said: «It is a true dream, InshaAllah. Go with Bilal and tell him what you saw, so that he can call the people to prayer with it, for he has a louder voice than you.» Umar ibn al-Khattab رضي الله عنه, when he heard, came out saying: "By the one Who sent you with the truth, O Messenger of Allah, I saw the same as he was shown!" And the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: «To Allah be praise.»

    🔊

    The Wording of the Adhan and Iqamah

    📿 Words of the Adhan — Narrated by Abu Dawud and Ibn Majah; sahih by al-Albani

    Allahu akbar, Allahu akbar, Allahu akbar, Allahu akbar; Ashhadu an laa ilaaha illa Allah, Ashhadu an laa ilaaha illa Allah; Ashhadu anna Muhammadan Rasoolullah, Ashhadu anna Muhammadan Rasoolullah; Hayya ala as-salat, Hayya ala as-salat; Hayya ala al-falah, Hayya ala al-falah; Allahu akbar, Allahu akbar; Laa ilaaha illa Allah.

    (Allah is Most Great x4; I bear witness there is no god except Allah x2; I bear witness Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah x2; Come to prayer x2; Come to prosperity x2; Allah is Most Great x2; There is no god worthy of worship except Allah)
    📿 Words of the Iqamah — Agreed Upon (Hadith of Anas)

    Allahu akbar, Allahu akbar; Ashhadu an laa ilaaha illa Allah; Ashhadu anna Muhammadan Rasoolullah; Hayya ala as-salat; Hayya ala al-falah; Qad qaamat is-salat, qad qaamat is-salat; Allahu akbar, Allahu akbar; Laa ilaaha illa Allah.

    The Prophet ﷺ instructed Bilal to say each phrase of the adhan twice and each phrase of the iqamah once, except "Qad qaamat is-salat" which is said twice.
    💡 Tathweeb (in Fajr adhan): After saying "Hayya ala al-falah" in the Fajr adhan, it is preferable to add: "As-salatu khayrun min an-nawm" (prayer is better than sleep), saying it twice. This is called tathweeb. Evidence: «If it is the adhan of Fajr, then say: As-salatu khayrun min an-nawm (prayer is better than sleep).» (Narrated by an-Nasai; classed as sahih by al-Albani)
    ✅

    Conditions for the Adhan to Be Valid — 3 Conditions

    • 1
      The Time for Prayer Should Have Begun

      It is not valid to call people to prayer before the time for the prayer has begun. Exception: the first adhan for Fajr and the first adhan for Jumuah prayer.

    • 2
      The Phrases Must Be Recited In Order and Consecutively

      As narrated in the Sunnah. It is Sunnah to pause briefly between the phrases of the adhan, and there is nothing wrong with it if the pause is slightly long.

    • 3
      The Adhan Must Be Said in Arabic

      In the wording narrated in the Sunnah.

    💡 It is not stipulated that when giving the adhan one must be in a state of purity, or turn to face towards the qiblah, or stand up for it, or refrain from speaking during it — although all of that is encouraged (mustahabb).
    👤

    Who May Give the Adhan — Stipulations for the Muadhdhin

    ⚖️ Basic Requirements

    The muadhdhin must be Muslim, of sound mind, and male. It is not valid for the adhan to be given by a disbeliever, one who is insane or drunk, or one who has not yet reached the age of discernment. It is also not valid for a woman to give the call to prayer to men.

    7 Desirable Qualities in the Muadhdhin:

    • 1
      Good Character and Trustworthy

      For he is in a position of trust and people refer to him with regard to prayer and fasting.

    • 2
      An Adult

      Although the adhan given by a boy who has reached the age of discernment is valid.

    • 3
      Knowledge of the Times of Prayer

      So that he can make sure that he gives the adhan as soon as the time for prayer comes.

    • 4
      A Pleasant Voice

      So that the call to prayer is appealing and audible.

    • 5
      In a State of Purity

      Free of minor or major impurity.

    • 6
      Give the Adhan Standing, Facing the Qiblah

      This is how the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions gave the adhan.

    • 7
      Recite the Adhan in an Unhurried Manner; Recite the Iqamah Quickly

      The adhan is recited slowly and clearly; the iqamah is delivered more quickly to signal prayer is immediately beginning.

    💡 Turning when saying "Hayya ala as-salat" and "Hayya ala al-falah": Turning right and left when saying these phrases is Sunnah. Evidence: Abu Juhayfah رضي الله عنه said: "When Bilal gave the adhan, I watched his mouth as he turned this way and that, right and left, saying: Hayya ala as-salat, Hayya ala al-falah." (al-Bukhari and Muslim). However, the one giving the adhan into a loudspeaker should not turn, because the sound comes from the minarets — turning would weaken the sound.
    🔁

    Repeating After the Muadhdhin

    📿 General Command — Agreed Upon

    «When you hear the call [to prayer], say what the muadhdhin says.»

    Agreed upon — Abu Saeed al-Khudri رضي الله عنه
    💡 For the phrases "Hayya ala as-salat" and "Hayya ala al-falah", the listener should say: «Laa hawla wa laa quwwata illa Billah» (There is no power and no strength except with Allah) instead of repeating these phrases.

    After the Adhan Ends:

    📿 Sending Blessings and Asking for Al-Waseelah — Narrated by Muslim

    «When you hear the muadhdhin, say what he says, then send blessings upon me, for whoever sends blessings upon me, Allah will send blessings upon him tenfold. Then ask Allah to grant me al-waseelah, for it is a station in Paradise which only one of the slaves of Allah will attain, and I hope that I will be the one. Whoever asks Allah for al-waseelah for me, intercession for him will be granted.»

    📿 Dua After the Adhan — Narrated by al-Bukhari

    «Whoever says when he hears the call to prayer, 'Allahumma Rabba hadhihi id-da'wat it-taammah was-salat il-qaa'imah, aati Muhammadan al-waseelata wa'l-fadeelah, wab'athu maqaman mahmoudan alladhi wa'adtahu' (O Allah, Lord of this perfect call and the prayer to be offered, grant Muhammad the privilege and also the eminence, and resurrect him to the praised position that You have promised), will be granted my intercession on the Day of Resurrection.»

    Narrated by al-Bukhari — Jabir ibn Abdillah رضي الله عنه
    💡 Dua between the adhan and iqamah is not rejected: «Supplication offered between the adhan and iqamah is not rejected.» (Narrated by Abu Dawud; classed as sahih by al-Albani — Anas ibn Malik رضي الله عنه)
    🎵

    The Virtue of the Adhan

    📿 Virtue of the First Row and the Call — Agreed Upon

    «If people knew what there is of virtue in the call [to prayer] and the first row, and could find no way except drawing lots for them, they would draw lots.»

    Agreed upon — Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه
    📿 The Muadhdhin on the Day of Resurrection — Narrated by Muslim

    «The muadhdhins will be the people with the longest necks on the Day of Resurrection.»

    Narrated by Muslim — Muawiyah رضي الله عنه
    🚫

    Innovations (Bidahs) and Mistakes in the Adhan

    Innovations (Bidahs):

    • Saying when the muadhdhin recites the shahadah: Azizullahi a'zam (What is dear to Allah is greatest).
    • Saying when the muadhdhin recites the iqamah: Nashhad (we bear witness) or Sadaqta (you have spoken the truth).
    • Saying when the muadhdhin says "As-salatu khayrun min an-nawm": Sadaqa Rasoolullahi sall Allah alayhi wa sallam (The Messenger of Allah ﷺ spoke the truth).
    ⚖️ Not Prescribed When the Iqamah Is Given

    It is not prescribed to say: Aqaamaha Allahu wa adaamaha (may Allah cause it to be always established and make it last), because the hadith which speaks of that is daeef (weak).

    Mistakes in the Phrases of the Adhan:

    • Elongating the first syllable of "Allahu akbar" in such a way that it forms a question in Arabic ("Is Allah greatest?") — this is a mistake.
    • Elongating the last syllable of "Allahu akbar" — this is a mistake.
    • Saying "Allah wa akbar" — this is a mistake.
    📋

    Related Issues — 6 Rulings

    • 1
      Repeating After Multiple Muadhdhins

      It is Sunnah to repeat after more than one muadhdhin until one offers the prayer. Once he has offered the prayer, it is not prescribed to repeat after any muadhdhin.

    • 2
      Adhan When Travelling

      Giving the adhan is prescribed for those who are travelling, because the Prophet ﷺ instructed Malik ibn al-Huwayrith رضي الله عنه and his companions to give the adhan when they were travelling.

    • 3
      Adhan and Women

      No adhan or iqamah is required of women, because they are not required to pray in congregation or to give the adhan. But if they give the adhan and iqamah without men hearing them, there is nothing wrong with that.

    • 4
      Joining Two Prayers Together

      When joining two prayers, the adhan should be given for the first prayer and the iqamah for each of them. Evidence: In Arafah, the Prophet ﷺ gave the adhan, then the iqamah and prayed Zuhr, then the iqamah and prayed Asr. In Muzdalifah, he gave the adhan, then the iqamah and prayed Maghrib, then the iqamah and prayed Isha. (Narrated by Muslim)

    • 5
      Who Gives the Iqamah

      It is better for the iqamah to be given by the same person who gave the adhan, although there is nothing wrong with someone else giving the iqamah, as there is no proof to disallow it. It is not Sunnah to repeat any of the words of the iqamah.

    • 6
      Television, Radio, and Recorded Adhan

      There is nothing wrong with repeating after a muadhdhin on the television or radio, if it is broadcast live. But if it is recorded, then it is not Sunnah to do that. A recorded adhan is not sufficient for calling people to prayer, because the adhan is a verbal act of worship which must be done by a person in real time.

    The Times of Prayer

    The obligatory prayers are to be offered five times every day and night. For each prayer there is a specific time, as defined in the religious texts.

    📖

    Overview — The Five Prayer Times

    «Indeed, prayer has been decreed upon the believers a decree of specified times»
    [an-Nisa 4:103]
    📿 Master Hadith — Times of All Five Prayers (Narrated by Muslim)

    «The time for Zuhr is when the sun passes the meridian, and a man's shadow is equal [in length] to his height, so long as the time for Asr has not come. The time for Asr is so long as the sun has not yet turned yellow. The time for Maghrib is so long as the red afterglow has not disappeared. The time for Isha is until half way through the night. The time for Fajr is from the break of dawn, so long as the sun has not yet risen.»

    Narrated by Muslim — Hadith of Abdullah ibn Amr رضي الله عنه
    ☀️

    1. The Time for Zuhr

    The time for Zuhr begins when the sun has passed the meridian — meaning it begins to decline from the highest point in the sky towards the west. Its time lasts until the shadow of a thing is equal [in length] to its height.

    💡 It is preferred to hasten to pray Zuhr when the time for it begins, unless it is extremely hot, in which case it is preferred to wait until it is cooler, because the Prophet ﷺ said: «If it is extremely hot, then delay prayer until it cools down, for extreme heat is from the exhalation of Hell.» (Agreed upon)
    🌤️

    2. The Time for Asr

    The time for Asr begins at the end of the time for Zuhr — i.e., when the shadow of a thing is equal [in length] to its height. The Sunnah is to hasten to pray at the beginning of the time for Asr. Its time lasts until the sun has set completely.

    «Maintain with care the [obligatory] prayers and [in particular] the middle prayer and stand before Allah, devoutly obedient»
    [al-Baqarah 2:238] — Asr is identified by many scholars as the "middle prayer"
    📿 Warning Against Missing Asr — al-Bukhari and Muslim

    «Whoever misses Asr prayer, it is as if he lost his family and his wealth.»

    📿 Severe Warning — al-Bukhari

    «Whoever misses Asr prayer, his deeds will be rendered invalid.»

    🌅

    3. The Time for Maghrib

    The time for Maghrib begins when the sun has set completely, until the red afterglow disappears.

    📚 What is the Red Afterglow?

    The red afterglow is the redness that appears in the sky after the sun has set and disappeared below the horizon. It lasts until the time for Isha begins.

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by Muslim

    «The time for Maghrib prayer lasts so long as the red afterglow has not disappeared.»

    ✅ It is Sunnah to hasten to pray Maghrib when the time for it begins: «My ummah will continue to be fine so long as they do not delay Maghrib until the stars begin to appear.» (Narrated by Abu Dawud; classed as sahih by al-Albani)
    🌙

    4. The Time for Isha

    The time for Isha begins when the time for Maghrib ends (when the red afterglow disappears) and ends after the first half of the night has passed.

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by Muslim

    «The time for Isha lasts until half way through the night.»

    ⚖️ Important Note

    The time for Isha ends halfway through the night, as defined in Islamic teaching — halfway between Maghrib and Fajr. It is not twelve midnight as ordinary people think. Praying Isha after half way through the night is regarded as making up the prayer (qada).

    🌄

    5. The Time for Fajr

    The time for Fajr lasts from the break of the true dawn until the sun rises.

    📿 Sunnah is to Pray Fajr Early — Agreed Upon

    Aisha رضي الله عنها said: "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to pray Fajr and the believing women would attend the prayer with him, wrapped in their outer garments, then they would go back to their houses and no one would recognize them because it was still so dark."

    ⚖️ Ruling on Delaying Fajr Until Sunrise

    It is not permissible to delay Fajr prayer deliberately until sunrise. Whoever prays it after the sun has risen, it is invalid and has not been done at the right time.

    Conditions of Prayer — 9 Conditions

    Conditions are prerequisites that must be met before and during prayer for it to be valid. Unlike essential parts, conditions exist outside the prayer itself.

    1️⃣

    Condition 1: Intention (Niyyah)

    📿 Evidence — Agreed Upon

    «Actions are but by intentions.»

    ⚠️ This is not waived under any circumstances. The place for the intention is in the heart, and it is not prescribed to utter it aloud, because the Prophet ﷺ did not utter it, and there is no report to indicate that any of his Companions did that either.
    2️⃣

    Condition 2: Being Muslim

    Prayer offered by a disbeliever is not valid, because his good deeds are invalid.

    «And what prevents their expenditures from being accepted from them but that they have disbelieved in Allah and in His Messenger»
    [at-Tawbah 9:54] — Disbelief in Allah renders good deeds invalid
    3️⃣

    Condition 3: Being of Sound Mind

    Prayer offered by one who is insane is not valid, because he is not accountable.

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by Abu Dawud; sahih by al-Albani

    «The Pen has been lifted from three: from the sleeping person until he wakes up, from the minor until he reaches puberty, and from the one who is insane until he comes to his senses.»

    4️⃣

    Condition 4: Being an Adult

    Prayer is not obligatory for a child until he reaches puberty, because of the hadith quoted above (the Pen has been lifted from three). However, children should be instructed to pray from age 7.

    5️⃣

    Condition 5: Being in a State of Purity — Free of Both Major and Minor Impurity

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by al-Bukhari

    «Allah does not accept the prayer of anyone of you if he is in a state of impurity, until he does wudu.»

    6️⃣

    Condition 6: The Time for Prayer Should Have Begun

    It is not valid to offer any prayer before the time for it begins, or after the time for it ends, unless one has an excuse.

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi; sahih by al-Albani

    Jibreel led the Prophet ﷺ in offering the five daily prayers, then he said: "The time [for each prayer] is between these two [times that he taught to the Prophet ﷺ]."

    7️⃣

    Condition 7: Covering the Awrah

    «O children of Adam, dress well every time you offer prayer»
    [al-A'raf 7:31]

    Men

    A man should put part of his garment over his shoulder: «No one of you should pray in a single garment of which no part is over his shoulder.» (an-Nasai; sahih by al-Albani)

    Women (in prayer)

    A woman's entire body is awrah except her face and hands: «Allah does not accept the prayer of a woman of childbearing age except with a headcover.» (Abu Dawud; sahih by al-Albani). If a woman prays in front of non-mahram men, she should cover everything, because «The woman is awrah.» (at-Tirmidhi; sahih by al-Albani)

    8️⃣

    Condition 8: Facing Towards the Qiblah

    «So turn your face toward al-Masjid al-Haram»
    [al-Baqarah 2:144]
    📿 Evidence — Agreed Upon

    «When you stand up to pray, do wudu properly, then turn to face towards the qiblah.»

    💡 If a Muslim can see the Kaaba, he must face it precisely. If he cannot see it, it is sufficient for him to face in its general direction.

    Praying in Airplanes, Cars and Ships:

    Supererogatory (Nafil) Prayer

    Permissible under any circumstances, whether or not one is able to stand or face the qiblah. The Prophet ﷺ used to offer supererogatory prayers on his mount, facing whatever direction his camel was facing. (al-Bukhari and Muslim). However, this is only permissible when travelling, not otherwise — it is one of the neglected Sunnahs.

    Obligatory Prayer

    If one will be able to catch up with the prayer on time when arriving, delay it. If one fears time will end, they may pray according to their situation — standing, bowing and prostrating if possible. Whatever one is unable to do is waived: «Pray standing, and if you cannot, then sitting, and if you cannot, then lying on your side.» (al-Bukhari). An-Nasai adds: «…and if you cannot, then lying on your back.»

    9️⃣

    Condition 9: Avoiding Impurities (Najasah) on Body, Clothing and Place of Prayer

    «And your clothing purify»
    [al-Muddaththir 74:4]
    • «Protect yourselves from urine, for most of the punishment in the grave is because of it.» (ad-Daraqutni; sahih by al-Albani)
    • «Scratch it, then rub it with water, then rinse it, then she may pray wearing it.» (al-Bukhari and Muslim — to Asma regarding menstrual blood on garment)
    • «Pour a bucket of water over his urine.» (al-Bukhari — when Bedouin urinated in mosque)

    Images Concealed in Pocket (e.g. money, ID)

    Scholars differed; the correct view is that it is permissible and not makruh.

    Images Carried Openly (e.g. on shirt/garment)

    It is not permissible to pray wearing it. The prayer is valid but the one who is aware of the ruling is sinning. Also not permissible to wear such things outside of prayer.

    ⚖️ Carrying a Medical Sample (e.g. urine bottle)

    It is not permissible to pray whilst carrying it — set it aside and pray without it. Exception: a catheter bag which the person needs and cannot remove — permissible to pray wearing it on condition it is closed firmly so nothing leaks. If it is easy to remove without harm, it must be done or the prayer is invalid.

    Essential Parts of Prayer (Arkān) — 12 Parts

    The essential parts (arkān) are the pillars of prayer. If any one of them is omitted — deliberately, by mistake, or out of ignorance — the prayer is invalid and cannot be corrected by doing the prostration of forgetfulness.

    1️⃣

    1. Standing (Qiyam)

    It is obligatory to stand in an obligatory prayer for the one who is able to do that.

    «and stand before Allah, devoutly obedient»
    [al-Baqarah 2:238]
    📿 Evidence — Narrated by al-Bukhari

    «Pray standing, and if you cannot, then sitting, and if you cannot, then lying on your side.»

    If Unable to Stand

    He may pray sitting or lying on his side. If he is able to stand but not able to prostrate or bow, then prostrating and bowing only are waived — he must still stand. Nothing is waived except what one is unable to do.

    Supererogatory Prayers

    It is permissible to pray sitting even when able to stand, although standing is preferable: «A man's prayer whilst sitting is equal to half a prayer.» (Narrated by Muslim)

    2️⃣

    2. The Opening Takbeer (Takbeerat al-Ihram)

    In order to begin the prayer, nothing is acceptable except saying takbeer ("Allahu akbar"). Prayer cannot be started without these words.

    📿 Evidence 1 — Agreed Upon

    «When you stand to pray, say takbeer.»

    📿 Evidence 2 — Narrated by Abu Dawud; sahih by al-Albani

    «Its beginning is takbeer and its end is tasleem.»

    3️⃣

    3. Reciting al-Fatihah in Every Rakah

    It is obligatory to recite it in full and correctly, in the correct order, without interruption.

    📿 Evidence — Agreed Upon

    «There is no prayer for the one who does not recite the Opening of the Book [i.e., al-Fatihah].»

    💡 It is obligatory for the worshipper to recite al-Fatihah in all cases — whether leading, praying behind an imam, or praying alone. Exception: one who joins the congregational prayer late and catches up with the imam when bowing — if not enough time, this requirement is waived.
    4️⃣

    4. Bowing (Ruku)

    «O you who have believed, bow»
    [al-Hajj 22:77]
    📿 Evidence — Agreed Upon (to the one who did not pray properly)

    «Then bow and pause in bowing.»

    5️⃣

    5. Rising from Bowing Until Standing Up Straight

    📿 Evidence — Agreed Upon

    «…then rise until you are standing up straight.»

    That means going back to the posture you were in before you bowed, whether you prayed standing or sitting. Aisha رضي الله عنها described the prayer of the Prophet ﷺ: «And when he lifted his head from bowing, he did not prostrate until he had stood up straight.» (Narrated by Muslim)

    6️⃣

    6. Prostration (Sujud)

    «and prostrate»
    [al-Hajj 22:77]
    📿 Evidence 1 — Agreed Upon

    «Then prostrate, and pause whilst prostrating.»

    📿 Evidence 2 — The Seven Bones — Agreed Upon

    «I have been commanded to prostrate on seven bones: the forehead — and he pointed to his nose — the two hands, the two knees, and the toes of the two feet.»

    7️⃣

    7. Rising from Prostration and Sitting Between the Two Prostrations

    📿 Evidence — Agreed Upon

    «Then rise and pause whilst sitting up.»

    8️⃣

    8. Pausing (Tuma'ninah) in Every Part of the Prayer

    📿 Evidence — Agreed Upon (hadith of the man who did not pray properly)

    «When you stand to pray, say takbeer then recite whatever you can of Quran, then bow and pause whilst bowing, then rise until you are standing up straight. Then prostrate and pause whilst prostrating, then rise and pause whilst sitting up, and do that throughout your entire prayer.»

    💡 Pausing (tuma'ninah) means pausing until all one's limbs settle in that posture, no matter how short a time that takes. It was also said that it means to pause for as long as it takes to recite the prescribed dhikr.
    9️⃣

    9. The Final Tashahhud and Sitting Therein

    📿 Evidence — an-Nasai; sahih by al-Albani

    Before the tashahhud was enjoined, Muslims used to say: As-salamu ala Allahi min ibadihi. But the Prophet ﷺ said: «Do not say 'As-salamu ala Allah'; rather say: 'At-tahiyyaatu Lillah…'»

    🔟

    10. Sending Blessings upon the Prophet ﷺ in the Final Tashahhud

    📿 Evidence — Agreed Upon

    «Say: Allahumma salli ala Muhammadin wa ala aali Muhammad… (O Allah send blessings upon Muhammad and upon the family of Muhammad…)»

    ⚠️ It is not appropriate to add to that and say "Allahumma salli ala Sayyidina Muhammad…" — this addition is an innovation.
    1️⃣1️⃣

    11. The Tasleem (Salutation at the End)

    📿 Evidence

    «It ends with the tasleem.»

    The worshipper should say to his right: "As-salamu alaykum wa rahmatullah" and to his left: "As-salamu alaykum wa rahmatullah." The Sunnah is to say the tasleem to the right and to the left, turning so far that the colour of one's cheek may be seen [from behind]. (Narrated by Muslim)

    1️⃣2️⃣

    12. Doing These Actions in Order

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by al-Bukhari

    «Pray as you have seen me praying.»

    The Prophet ﷺ did these actions in that order, and he taught the one who did not pray properly using the word «then (thumma)…», which indicates that these actions are to be done in order.

    ⚖️ Critical Rule

    These essential parts of the prayer are not waived, whether one omits them deliberately, by mistake or due to ignorance.

    Obligatory Parts of Prayer (Wājibāt) — 5 Parts

    The obligatory parts are of a lesser degree than the essential parts. If omitted deliberately the prayer is invalid; if omitted by mistake or ignorance, the prayer is not invalidated but the error must be corrected by doing the prostration of forgetfulness.

    ⚖️

    Key Distinction: Essential vs. Obligatory Parts

    Forgets an Essential Part

    Prayer is not valid unless he does it. Cannot be compensated for by prostration of forgetfulness.

    Forgets an Obligatory Part

    Acceptable to make up for it by doing the prostration of forgetfulness. This is what is indicated by the Sunnah.

    The evidence that these are obligatory is that the Prophet ﷺ always did these obligatory parts until he died, and he would do the prostration of forgetfulness if he forgot them.

    1️⃣

    Obligatory Part 1: The Takbeers of Moving from One Posture to Another

    These are all the takbeers apart from the opening takbeer. Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه used to say takbeer every time he moved down and up. When he finished praying, he said: "I am the one among you whose prayer most resembles that of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ." (Agreed upon)

    2️⃣

    Obligatory Part 2: Tasbeeh When Bowing and Prostrating

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by Muslim (Hudhayfah)

    "Then he bowed and started saying: Subhana Rabbiy al-Azeem (Glory be to my Lord the Most Great)… Then he prostrated and said: Subhana Rabbiy al-A'la (Glory be to my Lord the Most High)."

    📿 Evidence 2 — Narrated by Abu Dawud; hasan by an-Nawawi

    When the verse «So exalt the name of your Lord, the Most Great» [al-Waq'iah 56:74] was revealed, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: «Say it when you bow.» And when «Exalt the name of your Lord, the Most High» [al-A'la 87:1] was revealed, he said: «Say it when you prostrate.»

    💡 What is obligatory with regard to tasbeeh when bowing and prostrating is to say it once, but the Sunnah is to increase it to three, and the best is to say it ten times.
    3️⃣

    Obligatory Part 3: Tasmee and Tahmeed

    This means, respectively, that the imam and the one praying alone say when rising from bowing: Sami'a Allahu liman hamidah (Allah hears the one who praises Him), and when they have stood up straight, they say: Rabbana wa laka'l-hamd (Our Lord, and to You be praise).

    Imam and One Praying Alone

    Says both: Sami'a Allahu liman hamidah AND Rabbana wa laka'l-hamd.

    One Praying Behind an Imam

    It is sufficient to say the tahmeed only: Rabbana wa laka'l-hamd. Evidence: «When the imam says 'Sami'a Allahu liman hamidah,' then say: "Allahumma Rabbana laka'l-hamd"» (Agreed upon)

    4️⃣

    Obligatory Part 4: Saying "Rabb Ighfir Li" Between the Two Prostrations

    📿 Evidence — an-Nasai; sahih by al-Albani

    «Rabb ighfir li, Rabb ighfir li (Lord forgive me, Lord forgive me).»

    The Prophet ﷺ used to say this between the two prostrations — Hudhayfah رضي الله عنه
    5️⃣

    Obligatory Part 5: The First Tashahhud and Sitting For It

    📿 Evidence — an-Nasai; sahih by al-Albani

    «When one sits following each two rak'ahs, then say: 'At-tahiyyaatu Lillahi…'»

    📿 The Full Text of the Tashahhud — al-Bukhari and Muslim

    At-tahiyyaatu Lillahi wa's-salawatu wa't-tayyibat. As-salamu alayka ayyuha'n-Nabiyyu wa rahmatullahi wa barakatuhu. As-salamu alayna wa ala ibad-illah is-saliheen. Ashhadu an laa ilaaha illa Allah wa ashhadu anna Muhammadan abduhu wa rasooluh. (All compliments, prayers and pure words are due to Allah. Peace be upon you, O Prophet, and the mercy of Allah and His blessings. Peace be upon us and upon the righteous slaves of Allah. I bear witness that there is no god worthy of worship except Allah and I bear witness that Muhammad is His slave and Messenger).

    Sunnahs of Prayer — 18 Sunnahs

    The Sunnahs of prayer are recommended acts that complete and perfect the prayer. Omitting them does not invalidate the prayer, but performing them earns greater reward and more closely follows the Prophet's ﷺ example.

    1️⃣

    Sunnah 1: Raising the Hands — 4 Positions

    Raising the hands is done in four places: (1) when saying the opening takbeer, (2) when bowing, (3) when rising from bowing, and (4) when standing up following the first tashahhud.

    📿 Evidence — Agreed Upon (Ibn Umar رضي الله عنه)

    "I saw the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, when he started the prayer, raising his hands until they were level with his shoulders, and before he bowed, and when he rose from bowing, but he did not raise them between the two prostrations."

    📿 How to Raise the Hands — Narrated by Abu Dawud; sahih by al-Albani

    When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ started to pray, he would raise his hands, holding his fingers straight (not spread apart and not clenched).

    2️⃣

    Sunnah 2: Placing the Right Hand over the Left Hand Whilst Standing

    📿 Evidence (Sahl ibn Sa'd) — as-Sahihayn

    "The people were instructed that a man should place his right hand over his left forearm when praying."

    📿 Precise Position — Narrated by Abu Dawud; sahih by al-Albani

    Wa'il ibn Hujr رضي الله عنه said: "Then he placed his right hand over his left hand, wrist and forearm."

    💡 He may place his hands on his chest, or above his navel, or below it. The matter is broad in scope, because there is no proven hadith with regard to placement of the hands whilst standing.
    3️⃣

    Sunnah 3: Opening Supplication (Dua al-Istiftah)

    Before starting to recite al-Fatihah, the best is to recite one of the opening supplications proven in the Sunnah, choosing a different one each time. They include:

    📿 Dua 1 — Agreed Upon

    «Allahumma baa'id bayni wa bayna khataayyaaya kamaa baa'adta bayn al-mashriqi wa'l-maghrib. Allahumma naqqini min khataayayaaya kamaa yunaqqaa ath-thawb ul-abyad min ad-danas. Allahumma ighsilni min khataayayaaya bi'th-thalji wa'l-maa'i wa'l-barad» (O Allah, separate me (far) from my sins as You have separated (far) the East from the West. O Allah, cleanse me of my sins as white cloth is cleansed from dirt. O Allah, wash me of my sins with snow, water and ice.)

    📿 Dua 2 — Narrated by Abu Dawud; sahih by al-Albani

    «Subhanaka Allahumma wa bi hamdika, wa tabarak asmuka, wa ta'aala jadduka wa laa ilaaha ghayruka» (Glory and praise be to You, O Allah; blessed be Your name, exalted be Your Majesty, and there is no god worthy of worship except You.)

    📿 Dua 3 — Narrated by Muslim

    «Allahu akbar kabeera wa'l-hamdu Lillahi katheera wa subhanAllahi bukratan wa aseela…» (Allah is most Great, much praise be to Allah and glory be to Allah at the beginning and end of the day.)

    4️⃣

    Sunnah 4: Seeking Refuge with Allah (Ta'awwudh) Before Reciting Quran

    «So when you recite the Qur'an, [first] seek refuge in Allah from Satan, the expelled [from His mercy]»
    [an-Nahl 16:98]
    5️⃣

    Sunnah 5: The Basmalah

    It is Sunnah for the worshipper to recite Bismillah ir-Rahman ir-Raheem (in the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful) quietly before reciting al-Fatihah and before whatever follows al-Fatihah.

    ⚠️ It is not Sunnah to recite the Basmalah out loud when reciting al-Fatihah in prayer. At-Tirmidhi said: This was the practice of most of the scholars among the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ.
    6️⃣

    Sunnah 6: Ta'meen — Saying "Aameen"

    Ta'meen (saying "Aameen") is Sunnah for every worshipper at the end of al-Fatihah, whether he is leading the prayer, praying behind an imam, or praying alone. If the prayer is one in which recitation is done out loud, it is encouraged for the imam and those behind him to both say Aameen out loud.

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by al-Bukhari

    «When the imam says Aameen, then say Aameen, for the angels say Aameen, and if a person's saying Aameen coincides with that of the angels, his previous sins will be forgiven for him.»

    7️⃣

    Sunnah 7: Reciting Something in Addition to al-Fatihah

    📿 Evidence — Agreed Upon (Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه)

    «Whoever recites the Essence of the Book (al-Fatihah), that is sufficient for him, and whoever recites more than that, that is better.»

    8️⃣

    Sunnah 8: Reciting Out Loud and Reciting Quietly

    Recite Out Loud

    In Fajr prayer and in the first two rakahs of Maghrib and Isha.

    Recite Quietly

    In all other rakahs of the obligatory prayers (Zuhr, Asr, and the remaining rakahs of Maghrib and Isha).

    📿 Evidence — Agreed Upon

    «In every prayer there is recitation. What the Prophet ﷺ made us hear [by reciting out loud] we make you hear, and whatever he hid from us [by reciting quietly] we hide from you.»

    9️⃣

    Sunnah 9: Placing the Hands on the Knees with Fingers Spread Apart When Bowing, and Keeping the Back Straight with the Head in Line

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by Ibn Hibban; hasan by al-Albani

    «When you bow, place your palms on your knees, then spread your fingers apart.»

    Ibn Umar رضي الله عنه
    🔟

    Sunnah 10: Holding the Arms Away from the Sides When Bowing and Prostrating

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by Abu Dawud; sahih by al-Albani

    "And he [the Prophet ﷺ] put his hands on his knees and held his elbows away from his sides."

    Abu Humayd رضي الله عنه
    1️⃣1️⃣

    Sunnah 11: Reciting Extra Tasbeeh When Bowing and Prostrating

    Anas ibn Malik رضي الله عنه said: I have never seen anyone whose prayer more closely resembled that of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ than this young man — meaning Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz. We figured out that when he bowed, he said tasbeeh ten times, and when he prostrated he said tasbeeh ten times. (Narrated by Abu Dawud; hasan by al-Albani)

    1️⃣2️⃣

    Sunnah 12: Sitting in the Manner Called Iftirash

    This means that the worshipper sits on his left foot, which is spread on the ground, and holds his right foot upright, with the toes pointing in the direction of the qiblah.

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by al-Bukhari

    "The Sunnah when praying is to hold your right foot upright and bend the left leg." (Ibn Umar رضي الله عنه)

    💡 This manner of sitting is done when: sitting for the first tashahhud, sitting between the two prostrations, and sitting for the tashahhud in a two-rakah prayer. However, when sitting for the final tashahhud in a four-rakah prayer and Maghrib prayer, the worshipper should sit in the manner called tawarruk (see Sunnah 14).
    1️⃣3️⃣

    Sunnah 13: Sitting (Kneeling) with the Buttocks on the Feet Between Two Prostrations

    It was narrated that Tawus said: We said something to Ibn Abbas about sitting with the buttocks on the feet, and he said: "It is the Sunnah." We said: We think that it is difficult for a man. Ibn Abbas said: "Rather it is the Sunnah of your Prophet ﷺ." (Narrated by Muslim)

    Makruh Way (Dog-Sit)

    The buttocks are on the ground, the calves are held upright and the hands are placed on the ground, sitting as a dog sits. This is makruh and there is a prohibition concerning it.

    Correct Sunnah Way

    The buttocks rest on the heels, between the two prostrations. This is what Ibn Abbas meant by "the Sunnah of your Prophet ﷺ."

    1️⃣4️⃣

    Sunnah 14: Sitting in the Manner Called Tawarruk (Final Tashahhud)

    It is Sunnah to sit in this manner in the final tashahhud in a prayer in which there are two tashahhuds. It may be done in more than one way (all three ways are mentioned in the Sunnah):

    • First way: The left foot protrudes from under the right thigh, resting on the ground, and the person sits on his buttocks, holding the right foot upright.
    • Second way: Both feet rest on the ground, and are both held out on one side.
    • Third way: The right foot rests on the ground, the person places his left foot between the thigh and calf [of his right leg] and sits on his buttocks.
    1️⃣5️⃣

    Sunnah 15: Pointing with the Forefinger When Reciting the Tashahhud

    It was narrated from Ibn Umar رضي الله عنه that when the Messenger of Allah ﷺ sat to recite the tashahhud, he placed his left hand on his left knee, and he placed his right hand on his right knee, making a shape with his fingers that could indicate the number fifty-three, and he pointed with his forefinger. (Narrated by Muslim)

    📿 Virtue of the Forefinger — Narrated by Ahmad; sahih by al-Albani

    «Indeed it is harder on the Shaytan than iron» — meaning the forefinger [used as a pointer].

    1️⃣6️⃣

    Sunnah 16: Looking at the Forefinger Whilst Reciting the Tashahhud

    It is proven from Abdullah ibn Umar رضي الله عنه that he saw a man moving pebbles with his hand whilst praying. When he finished praying, Abdullah said: "Do not move pebbles whilst you are praying, for that is from the Shaytan. Rather you should do what the Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to do." The man asked: "What did he use to do?" He said: "He used to place his right hand on his right thigh and point towards the qiblah with the finger that is next to the thumb, looking at it." (an-Nasai; sahih by al-Albani)

    ⚖️ Prohibition — Looking Up to Heaven

    Looking up to heaven is prohibited and is in fact a major sin, because the Prophet ﷺ forbade it and spoke sternly about it: «People should stop lifting their gaze to heaven whilst praying, or [their sight] will not come back to them.» According to another report: «…or their sight will be snatched away from them.» (al-Bukhari and Muslim)

    1️⃣7️⃣

    Sunnah 17: Supplication Following the Tashahhud and Before the Tasleem

    📿 Evidence — an-Nasai; sahih by al-Albani

    «When you sit after every two rakahs, say: 'At-tahiyyaatu Lillahi wa's-salawatu wa't-tayyibat. As-salamu alayka ayyuha'n-Nabiyyu wa rahmatullahi wa barakatuhu. As-salamu alayna wa ala ibad-illah is-saliheen. Ashhadu an laa ilaaha illa Allah wa ashhadu anna Muhammadan abduhu wa rasoolohu.' Then let him choose whatever supplication he likes and call upon Allah ﷻ with it.»

    1️⃣8️⃣

    Sunnah 18: Turning Right and Left When Saying the Tasleem

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by Muslim

    Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas رضي الله عنه said: "I used to see the Messenger of Allah ﷺ saying the tasleem to his right and his left, turning so much that I could see the whiteness of his cheek."

    Description of Prayer — Step by Step

    A complete walkthrough of how the Prophet ﷺ performed the prayer, from the opening intention to the final tasleem, including the dhikr after prayer and the sutrah ruling.

    🕌

    Before the Prayer Begins — The Right Frame of Mind

    💡 When the worshipper starts to pray, he should realize that he is standing before Allah ﷻ and that he is conversing with Him. If he bears that in mind, he will begin his prayer with his heart filled with reverence of Allah ﷻ, love of Him and longing to draw close to Him.
    1️⃣

    Step 1: Opening Takbeer and Initial Posture

    • Say "Allahu akbar" whilst raising the hands until they are level with the shoulders or with the earlobes.
    • Put the right hand over the left hand and tilt the head (looking at the place of prostration), not lifting his head to look up to heaven.
    • Recite the opening supplication (dua al-istiftah).
    • Seek refuge with Allah (ta'awwudh).
    • Say Bismillah.
    • Recite al-Fatihah, followed by another surah.
    📖

    What Surahs to Recite — Al-Mufassal

    📚 Al-Mufassal

    Al-Mufassal begins with Surat Qaf, up to an-Nas. It is called al-Mufassal because its verses are short, with many breaks (fawasil).

    Long Surahs of al-Mufassal

    From Qaf to an-Naba (Amma). It is Sunnah to recite these in Fajr prayer — and sometimes to recite the short surahs such as az-Zalzalah and al-Qadr, and sometimes to recite the medium-length surahs.

    Medium-Length Surahs

    From an-Naba to ad-Duha.

    Short Surahs

    From ad-Duha to the end of the Quran. It is Sunnah to recite these in Maghrib prayer — and sometimes to recite long surahs such as al-A'raf and at-Tur.

    2️⃣

    Step 2: Bowing (Ruku)

    • Raise the hands and say takbeer for bowing, as he raised his hands when he said the opening takbeer.
    • Place the hands on the knees with fingers spread apart, holding out the elbows from the sides.
    • Make the back and head in a straight line, with no bend.
    • Say "Subhana Rabbiy al-Azeem (Glory be to my Lord the Most Great)" repeating it three times, and glorify Allah ﷻ a great deal whilst bowing.
    3️⃣

    Step 3: Rising from Bowing

    • Raise the head and say: "Sami'a Allahu liman hamidah" raising the hands until they are level with the shoulders or earlobes.
    • After standing up straight, say: "Rabbana wa laka'l-hamd" or "Rabbana wa laka'l-hamd" or "Allahumma Rabbana laka'l-hamd" or "Allahumma Rabbana wa laka'l-hamd."
    📿 Full Dua After Rising from Ruku — Narrated by Muslim

    "…mil' as-samawaati wa mil' al-ardi wa mil'a ma baynahuma wa mil'a ma shi'ta min shay'in ba'dahu. Ahl al-thana' wa'l-majd, ahaqqu ma qaala al-abd, wa kulluna laka abd. Laa maani'a lima a'tayta wa la mu'tiya lima mana'ta wa laa yanfa'u dha'l-jaddi minka al-jadd (… filling the heavens, filling the earth, filling everything in between and filling whatever else You will besides that. You are the One Who most deserves praise and glorification, (this is) the soundest thing a slave could say, and we are all Your slaves. None can withhold what You give and none can give what You withhold, and the good fortune of any fortunate person is to no avail against Your punishment)."

    4️⃣

    Step 4: Prostration (Sujud)

    • Say takbeer and prostrate without raising the hands.
    • Prostrate on the seven parts: the forehead along with the nose, the two hands, the two knees, and the toes of the two feet.
    • Hold the forearms up and not rest them on the ground.
    • Hold the upper arms away from the sides, the stomach away from the thighs, and the thighs away from the calves.
    • Do not elongate the back like someone lying on his face.
    • Say "Subhana Rabbiy al-A'la" three times, and offer a great deal of supplication whilst prostrating.
    📿 Virtue of Dua in Sujud — Narrated by Muslim

    «With regard to prostration, offer a great deal of supplication in it, for it is more deserving of receiving a response.»

    5️⃣

    Step 5: Sitting Between the Two Prostrations

    • Rise from prostration, saying takbeer, and sit in the manner called iftirash.
    • Place the right hand on the right thigh (or kneecap) and the left hand on the left thigh.
    • Say: "Rabb ighfir li, Rabb ighfir li (Lord forgive me, Lord forgive me)."
    • Then prostrate a second time, saying the same as in the first prostration.
    6️⃣

    Step 6: The Second Rakah and Tashahhud

    • Stand up for the second rakah, saying takbeer.
    • In the second rakah, do the same as in the first, without the opening supplication.
    • After the second rakah, sit to recite the tashahhud in the manner called iftirash.
    • It is Sunnah to curl the pinky finger and ring finger, make a circle with the thumb and the middle finger, and point with the forefinger whilst reciting the tashahhud, focusing the gaze on the forefinger.
    • Recite the tashahhud — several versions have been narrated; he may choose whichever he likes, but the best is to vary between them.
    7️⃣

    Step 7: Continuing to 3 or 4 Rakahs

    • If offering a three- or four-rakah prayer, stand up after reciting the first tashahhud, raising the hands as when saying the opening takbeer.
    • Offer the rest of the prayer, reciting al-Fatihah only — he does not have to recite another surah with it. But if he recites another surah sometimes, there is nothing wrong with that, as it is proven that the Prophet ﷺ sometimes did that.
    • Then sit for the second tashahhud in the manner called tawarruk (which differs from iftirash).
    8️⃣

    Step 8: The Final Tashahhud and Closing

    Recite the final tashahhud, then add the following (not in the first tashahhud):

    📿 Salawat Ibrahimiyyah — al-Bukhari and Muslim

    "Allahumma salli ala Muhammadin wa ala aali Muhammadin kama salayta ala Ibrahima wa ala aali Ibrahim, innaka hameedun majeed. Allahumma barik ala Muhammadin wa ala aali Muhammadin kama barakta ala Ibrahima wa ala aali Ibrahim innaka hameedun majeed (O Allah, send prayers upon Muhammad and upon the family of Muhammad, as You sent prayers upon Ibrahim and upon the family of Ibrahim; You are indeed Worthy of Praise, Full of Glory. O Allah, send blessings upon Muhammad and upon the family of Muhammad as You sent blessings upon the family of Ibrahim; You are indeed Worthy of Praise, Full of Glory)."

    📿 Dua Before Tasleem — Narrated by Muslim

    "A'oodhu Billahi min adhaabi Jahannam wa min adhaabi'l-qabri wa min fitnat il-mahyaa wa'l-mamaat wa min fitnat il-masih id-dajjal (I seek refuge with Allah from the punishment of Hell and the punishment of the grave, and from the trials of life and death and from the trial of the Dajjal)."

    He may then offer supplication for whatever he wants of the good of this world and the Hereafter. Then he should say the tasleem to his right and to his left, turning his face so far to the right and left that the colour of his cheek can be seen [from behind]. Thus the prayer ends.

    🤲

    Dhikr Following Prayer

    «And when you have completed the prayer, remember Allah standing, sitting, or [lying] on your sides»
    [an-Nisa 4:103]
    • Say after the tasleem: "Laa ilaaha illa Allah wahdahu laa shareeka lah, lahu'l-mulk wa lahu'l-hamd wa huwa ala kulli shay'in qadeer. Allahumma laa maani'a lima a'tayta wa la mu'tiya lima mana'ta wa laa yanfa'u dha'l-jaddi minka al-jadd" (There is no god worthy of worship except Allah Alone, with no partner or associate. His is the sovereignty and to Him be praise, and He is Able to do all things. O Allah, none can withhold what You give and none can give what You withhold, and the good fortune of any fortunate person is to no avail against Your punishment). (al-Bukhari and Muslim)
    • Ask Allah for forgiveness three times and say: "Allahumma anta as-salam wa minka as-salam tabarakta yaa dhal-jalali wa'l-ikram" (O Allah, You are the One Who is free from all defects and deficiencies and from You is all peace, blessed are You, O Possessor of majesty and honour). (Narrated by Muslim)
    • Glorify Allah, praise Him and magnify Him thirty-three times following every prayer. (as-Sahihayn)
    • Recite the Mu'awwidhatain (al-Falaq and an-Nas) following every prayer. (Narrated by at-Tirmidhi; sahih by al-Albani — Uqbah ibn Aamir رضي الله عنه)
    • Ka'b ibn Ujrah رضي الله عنه narrated that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Dhikrs after each obligatory prayer will not be disappointing — thirty-three tasbeehahs (Subhan Allah), thirty-three tahmeedahs (Alhamdulillah) and thirty-four takbeerahs (Allahu Akbar)." (Narrated by Muslim)
    ⚖️ Raising the Hands After Prayer

    It is not prescribed to raise the hands when offering supplication after prayer, because there is no report to that effect from the Prophet ﷺ.

    🚧

    Sutrah (Screen) When Praying

    📚 What is a Sutrah?

    A sutrah is a screen or barrier placed in front of the worshipper whilst praying — such as a wall, a pillar, or a stick placed in the ground.

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by Abu Dawud and Ibn Majah; sahih by al-Albani

    «When one of you prays, let him pray facing a sutrah and let him stand close to it.»

    Abu Saeed al-Khudri رضي الله عنه

    Actions That Are Disliked (Makruh) in Prayer — 8 Actions

    These actions do not invalidate the prayer but reduce its reward and completeness. The worshipper should avoid them to perfect their prayer.

    1️⃣

    Makruh 1: Turning Slightly and Unnecessarily

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by al-Bukhari

    The Prophet ﷺ was asked about turning whilst praying: «That is something that the Shaytan steals from a person's prayer.»

    The word "stealing" here refers to taking something discreetly or openly. But if one turns for a valid reason, there is nothing wrong with it.

    Turning Whilst Praying — 3 Categories:

    Category 1 — Invalidates the Prayer

    Turning with the whole body to the point that one turns away completely from the qiblah. This invalidates the prayer.

    Category 2 — Disliked (Makruh)

    Turning one's chest whilst one's feet remain facing towards the qiblah. This is disliked (makruh), and some scholars stated that it invalidates the prayer.

    Category 3 — Disliked Unless Necessary

    Turning one's head or eyes right and left. This is disliked, unless it is for a reason.

    2️⃣

    Makruh 2: Resting the Forearms on the Ground When Prostrating

    📿 Evidence — Agreed Upon

    «Prostrate properly; no one of you should rest his forearms on the ground like a dog.»

    3️⃣

    Makruh 3: Tucking (Tying) Up One's Hair or Garment

    📿 Evidence — Agreed Upon

    «I have been commanded to prostrate on seven, and not to tuck (or tie) up my hair or garment.»

    Hadith of Ibn Abbas رضي الله عنه
    💡 This refers to gathering up the hair or garment, preventing them from prostrating with the person when he prostrates.
    4️⃣

    Makruh 4: Putting the Hands on the Hips

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by al-Bukhari

    "The Prophet ﷺ forbade a man to pray with his hands on his hips."

    Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه
    💡 Praying with hands on the hips is not allowed because it is what the Jews do, and because this posture is not appropriate for the worshipper who is standing before Allah ﷻ.
    5️⃣

    Makruh 5: Covering the Mouth Whilst Praying

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi; sahih by al-Albani

    "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ forbade a man to cover his mouth."

    Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه
    6️⃣

    Makruh 6: Interlacing the Fingers

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by Abu Dawud; sahih by al-Albani

    «When one of you does wudu and does it well, then goes out heading towards the mosque, let him not interlace his fingers, for he is in a state of prayer.»

    💡 This applies outside of prayer as well — it is more disliked when one is actually praying. As for interlacing the fingers at times other than the time of prayer, it is not disliked, even if one is in the mosque, because the Prophet ﷺ did that in the story of Dhu'l-Yadayn narrated in as-Sahihayn.
    7️⃣

    Makruh 7: Moving a Great Deal and Fidgeting During Prayer

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by Muslim

    «Be still in prayer.»

    Categories of Movement During Prayer — 5 Rulings:

    • 1
      Obligatory

      Moving when the validity of the prayer depends on it — such as if someone tells the worshipper that he is not facing the qiblah. In that case he must move to face towards it.

    • 2
      Prohibited

      A great deal of consecutive movement with no need for that.

    • 3
      Encouraged

      Moving in order to do something that is encouraged (mustahabb) in the prayer — such as if one sees a gap in the row in front, and steps forward to fill it.

    • 4
      Permissible

      Slight movement for which there is a need, or moving a great deal in the case of necessity.

    • 5
      Disliked

      Slight movement for which there is no need.

    8️⃣

    Makruh 8: Praying When Food Is Ready or Resisting the Urge to Relieve Oneself

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by Muslim

    «There is no prayer when food is ready or when one is resisting the urge to relieve oneself [by urinating or defecating].»

    ⚖️ When This Applies

    This applies in the case where the individual feels that he is longing to eat and is able to eat the food, and it is in front of him and ready to eat. However, if the food is ready but he is fasting, or he is full and does not want to eat, or he is not able to eat it because it is too hot — in all of these cases it is not disliked for him to pray when the food is ready.

    Things That Invalidate the Prayer — 11 Invalidators

    These are the actions and states that, if they occur deliberately during prayer, render the prayer null and void and require it to be repeated.

    1️⃣

    Invalidator 1: Wudu Becoming Invalidated

    ⚠️ Being in a state of purity is a condition of the prayer being valid. So if that state of purity becomes invalid, the prayer becomes invalid.
    2️⃣

    Invalidator 2: Impure Substance (Najasah) Coming in Contact with the Worshipper

    If najasah comes in contact with him and he is aware of it, and he does not remove it immediately when able to do so, the prayer becomes invalid. If he is not able to remove it whilst praying, he must exit the prayer.

    💡 Example: If he remembers there is something impure on his shemagh (head cover), he should take it off. But if there is some impurity on his underwear and he remembers it, he must exit the prayer in order to remove it.
    3️⃣

    Invalidator 3: Uncovering the Awrah Deliberately

    Covering the awrah is a condition of the prayer. Deliberately uncovering it invalidates the prayer.

    4️⃣

    Invalidator 4: Turning One's Back Towards the Qiblah Deliberately

    Facing the qiblah is a condition of the prayer. Deliberately turning one's back towards it invalidates the prayer.

    5️⃣

    Invalidator 5: Speaking Deliberately

    📿 Evidence — as-Sahihayn (Zayd ibn Arqam رضي الله عنه)

    We used to speak during the prayer; one of us would speak to his companion when he was next to him during the prayer, until the verse was revealed: «and stand before Allah, devoutly obedient» [al-Baqarah 2:238]. Then we were commanded to remain silent and were forbidden to speak.

    6️⃣

    Invalidator 6: Laughing Out Loud

    Laughing out loud invalidates the prayer according to scholarly consensus.

    Laughing Is of Three Levels:

    1. Smiling

    Does not render the prayer invalid.

    2. Laughing Out Loud

    Renders the prayer invalid in all cases, according to scholarly consensus, because it involves making loud noises contrary to what is appropriate whilst praying, and is showing disrespect towards the prayer.

    3. Chuckling (Laughing Quietly)

    Something between smiling and laughing out loud. The majority of fuqaha are of the view that the prayer is rendered invalid by chuckling if two sounds are made, otherwise it does not invalidate it.

    7️⃣

    Invalidator 7: Passage of an Adult Woman, a Donkey, or a Black Dog in Front of the Worshipper

    This applies when they pass directly in front of the worshipper, closer than the place in which he prostrates.

    📿 Evidence — Narrated by Muslim

    «When one of you stands to pray, he will be screened if he has something in front of him that is like the back of a saddle. If he does not have something in front of him that is like the back of a saddle, then his prayer will be interrupted if a donkey, a woman or a black dog passes in front of him.»

    8️⃣

    Invalidator 8: Omitting an Essential Part, Condition or Obligatory Part Deliberately

    ⚠️ Deliberately omitting any essential part, condition, or obligatory part of the prayer with no excuse invalidates the prayer.
    9️⃣

    Invalidator 9: Eating and Drinking Deliberately

    ⚖️ Scholarly Consensus

    Ibn al-Mundhir said: The scholars are unanimously agreed that the one who deliberately eats or drinks during an obligatory prayer must repeat it.

    🔟

    Invalidator 10: Deliberately Adding an Essential Part, or Doing Essential Parts Out of Order

    Deliberately adding an essential part (such as an extra ruku or sujud), or doing some essential parts before others (out of the prescribed order), invalidates the prayer.

    1️⃣1️⃣

    Invalidator 11: Cancelling the Intention

    ⚠️ Forming the intention and keeping it in mind is a condition of the prayer being valid. If one cancels the intention during the prayer, the prayer becomes invalid.
