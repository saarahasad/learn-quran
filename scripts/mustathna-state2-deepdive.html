This is the full working-out of State 2 — what happens when the speech before إِلَّا is both تَامّ (complete: the mustathnā minhu is mentioned) and مَنْفِيّ (negative: preceded by a negation). Unlike State 1, where naṣb is the only option, here the grammarian is handed a genuine choice — and the commentary explains exactly why that choice exists and why one option is used far more often than the other.

1Confirming why State 2 applies سَبَبُ ثُبُوتِ الْحَالَةِ الثَّانِيَةِ

مَا قَامَ الْقَوْمُ إِلَّا زَيْدٌ "The people did not stand, except Zayd."
complete? تَامّ ✓ الْقَوْمُ (the mustathnā minhu) is stated
negative? مَنْفِيّ ✓ preceded by مَا, the particle of negation

Both conditions for State 2 are satisfied at once, which is exactly why two grammatical options open up for the word زيد — a choice that never arises in State 1's affirmative sentences.

2Option 1 — following as a substitute الْإِتْبَاعُ عَلَى الْبَدَلِيَّةِ the usual choice

مَا قَامَ الْقَوْمُ إِلَّا زَيْدٌ زَيْدٌ here is a بَدَل (substitute/replacement) for الْقَوْمُ — it grammatically "follows" it rather than being governed by إلا.

The reasoning is a short, clean syllogism, stated explicitly in the text:

الْمُسْتَثْنَى مِنْهُ مَرْفُوعٌ the mustathnā minhu (القوم) is marfūʿ
بَدَلُ الْمَرْفُوعِ مَرْفُوعٌ → a substitute for a marfūʿ word is itself marfūʿ

iʿrāb — reading 1 (al-badl)

مَا Particle of negation, mabnī, no locus of iʿrāb.
قَامَ Māḍī verb, mabnī on a fatḥah.
الْقَوْمُ Fāʿil of قَامَ, marfūʿ — and the مُسْتَثْنَى مِنْهُ that زيد will be substituted for.
إِلَّا Particle of exception, mabnī, no locus of iʿrāb. Here it does not itself govern زيد into naṣb.
زَيْدٌ Badal (بَدَلُ بَعْضٍ مِنْ كُلٍّ — substitute of a part from a whole) from الْقَوْمُ, marfūʿ following it, sign of rafʿ the visible ḍammah.

Sense of this reading: the negation is really about الْقَوْمُ as a whole, and زيدٌ is then substituted in as the one true exception to that negation — so the case of زيد simply mirrors the case its "replacement target" القوم already carries, rather than being assigned fresh by إلا.

3Option 2 — manṣūb as exception النَّصْبُ عَلَى الِاسْتِثْنَاءِ permissible, but rare

مَا قَامَ الْقَوْمُ إِلَّا زَيْدًا The same sentence, second reading: زَيْدًا treated as an ordinary mustathnā, governed into naṣb by إلا — structurally identical to State 1, even though the sentence here happens to be negative.

iʿrāb — reading 2 (al-istithnāʾ)

زَيْدًا Mustathnā, manṣūb by إلا, sign of naṣb the visible fatḥah. (The rest of the sentence — مَا، قَامَ، الْقَوْمُ، إِلَّا — is parsed exactly as in reading 1.)

The text is explicit that this option is عَلَى قِلَّةٍ — "of scarcity," meaning grammatically valid and attested, but used far less often in practice than the badal reading. In everyday and classical usage, a negative-complete sentence like this one is overwhelmingly more likely to be voiced with زيدٌ (badal, marfūʿ) than with زيدًا (istithnāʾ, manṣūb).

5A comparable Qurʾānic instance

211 وَمَا يُؤْمِنُ أَكْثَرُهُمْ بِاللَّهِ إِلَّا وَهُمْ مُّشْرِكُونَ Sūrah Yūsuf, 12:106 — "And most of them believe not in Allāh except while they associate others with Him [in worship]." The commentator cites this verse alongside مَا قَامَ الْقَوْمُ إِلَّا زَيْدٌ as a further Qurʾānic instance of إلا appearing within a negative, complete sentence — the same negative environment (a preceding مَا) that makes the State-2 flexibility available in ordinary examples like the one above.

In short: once a sentence before إلا is both complete and negative, its excepted noun stops being locked into naṣb. It may instead follow the case of the mustathnā minhu as a badal (the far more common choice — "بدل المرفوع مرفوع"), or it may still take naṣb as a straightforward mustathnā (grammatically sound, but described as عَلَى قِلَّة — rarely used). Both مَا قَامَ الْقَوْمُ إِلَّا زَيْدٌ and مَا قَامَ الْقَوْمُ إِلَّا زَيْدًا are correct Arabic; they simply reflect two different underlying analyses of the same sentence.
