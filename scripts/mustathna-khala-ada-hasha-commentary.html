The last three tools of exception — خَلَا، عَدَا، and حَاشَا — are the "flexible" family flagged at the very start of this chapter: sometimes particles, sometimes genuine past-tense verbs. That dual identity is exactly what produces this section's headline rule — the noun after them can take either of two case endings, and which one you pick depends entirely on which identity you mentally assign the word in that instance.

1The core dual rule يَجُوزُ النَّصْبُ وَالْجَرُّ

Ibn Ājurrūm states it plainly: وَالْمُسْتَثْنَى بِـ خَلَا وَعَدَا وَحَاشَا يَجُوزُ نَصْبُهُ وَجَرُّهُ — "the word excepted with khalā, ʿadā, and ḥāshā may be either manṣūb or majrūr."

قَامَ الْقَوْمُ خَلَا زَيْدًا / زَيْدٍ
... عَدَا عَمْرًا / عَمْرٍو
... حَاشَا بَكْرًا / بَكْرٍ

Every one of the three follows the identical pattern — a manṣūb form and a majrūr form, both correct:

ToolManṣūb formMajrūr form
خَلَازَيْدًازَيْدٍ
عَدَاعَمْرًاعَمْرٍو
حَاشَابَكْرًابَكْرٍ

2The secret behind the choice السِّرُّ فِي ذَلِكَ

السِّرُّ فِي ذَلِكَ أَنَّ هَذِهِ الْأَدَوَاتِ تُسْتَعْمَلُ أَفْعَالًا تَارَةً وَتُسْتَعْمَلُ حُرُوفًا تَارَةً أُخْرَى — the reason is that these three words are used sometimes as verbs and sometimes as particles. Each identity carries its own consequence for the noun that follows:

إِنْ قُدِّرَتْ فِعْلًاread as a verb
The following noun becomes مَفْعُولٌ بِهِ (object), manṣūb. The فَاعِل (subject) is a hidden pronoun, obligatorily concealed (ضَمِيرٌ مُسْتَتِرٌ وُجُوبًا) — roughly "he was apart from Zayd," with "he" referring back to the mustathnā minhu.
إِنْ قُدِّرَتْ حَرْفًاread as a particle
The following noun becomes مَجْرُورٌ, governed into jarr by the particle itself — functioning exactly like a preposition-style exception tool, no verbal sense involved at all.

iʿrāb of قَامَ الْقَوْمُ خَلَا زَيْدًا (verb reading)

قَامَ Māḍī verb, mabnī on a fatḥah.
الْقَوْمُ Fāʿil of قَامَ, marfūʿ.
خَلَا Māḍī verb, mabnī on a fatḥah (taqdīran — implied, since alif maqṣūrah blocks the visible fatḥah). Its فاعل is a hidden pronoun (هُوَ), obligatorily concealed, referring back to القوم.
زَيْدًا Mafʿūl bihi of خَلَا, manṣūb, sign of naṣb the visible fatḥah.

iʿrāb of قَامَ الْقَوْمُ خَلَا زَيْدٍ (particle reading)

خَلَا Particle of exception (حَرْفُ اسْتِثْنَاءٍ), mabnī, no locus of iʿrāb.
زَيْدٍ Majrūr by خَلَا, sign of jarr the visible kasrah.

Both parsings describe exactly the same sentence and mean the same thing — they only disagree on whether خلا is functioning grammatically as a concealed-subject verb or as a bare preposition-like particle in that instance. Since both readings are always legitimate wherever this rule applies, the speaker is free to choose either.

3The exception to the exception — "مَا" of the infinitive مَا الْمَصْدَرِيَّةُ

This dual freedom is not unconditional. The commentary closes with an important restriction: the choice only exists when خلا/عدا/حاشا are not preceded by مَا الْمَصْدَرِيَّة ("the mā of the infinitive"). If this مَا does precede them, the noun after them must be manṣūb — no alternative is permitted.

without مَا قَامَ الْقَوْمُ خَلَا زَيْدٍ / زَيْدًا both readings possible
with مَا المصدرية قَامَ الْقَوْمُ مَا خَلَا زَيْدًا manṣūb only — obligatory

The reasoning given is grammatically tight: مَا الْمَصْدَرِيَّة is a particle that only ever attaches to a verb — it never precedes a particle. So the moment مَا appears directly before خلا (or عدا), it forces خلا to be read purely as a verb; the particle-reading is no longer available, and with it goes the jarr option. Only the verb-reading survives, which means the following noun is locked into being its mafʿūl bihi — manṣūb, with no choice left.

iʿrāb of قَامَ الْقَوْمُ مَا خَلَا زَيْدًا

قَامَ الْقَوْمُ Parsed exactly as before — māḍī verb + fāʿil, marfūʿ.
مَا مَا الْمَصْدَرِيَّة ("the mā of the infinitive"), mabnī, no locus of iʿrāb — its only function is to force what follows it to be read as a verb.
خَلَا Māḍī verb, mabnī on an implied fatḥah — now obligatorily a verb, since مَا cannot precede a particle. Its fāʿil is the same obligatorily-hidden pronoun as before.
زَيْدًا Mafʿūl bihi of خَلَا, manṣūb, sign of naṣb the visible fatḥah — the only possible reading; جر (jarr) is not permitted here at all.

In short: خلا، عدا، and حاشا are unique among the eight exception-tools in offering a genuine either/or on the case of the following noun — manṣūb if you read the tool as a concealed-subject verb (noun = mafʿūl bihi), or majrūr if you read it as a bare particle (noun = majrūr by it). That freedom disappears the instant مَا الْمَصْدَرِيَّة is placed directly before the tool: since مَا only ever attaches to verbs, its presence forces the verb-reading exclusively, and the following noun must then be manṣūb with no alternative.
