This is the full working-out of State 3 — the case that applies when the speech before إِلَّا is نَاقِص (incomplete: the mustathnā minhu is never mentioned), which the commentary already established always co-occurs with negation. Here the choice-based flexibility of State 2 disappears entirely, replaced by a single mechanical rule: let the sentence's own governor decide.

1The governing rule الْقَاعِدَةُ الْعَامَّة

كَانَ الْمُسْتَثْنَى عَلَى حَسَبِ مَا قَبْلَ إِلَّا مِنَ الْعَوَامِلِ — "the excepted noun follows whatever governor precedes إلا dictates." Since the mustathnā minhu was never stated, there is no "whole" for the excepted noun to be pulled out of or substituted for — so إلا effectively steps aside, and the noun after it is simply assigned whatever case the sentence's own verb (or preposition) would assign it if إلا weren't there at all. Three outcomes follow, one for each type of governor:

رَفْعُ الْفَاعِلِيَّةِ governor requires a fāʿil → marfūʿ
نَصْبُ الْمَفْعُولِيَّةِ governor requires a mafʿūl bihi → manṣūb
الْجَرُّ بِحَرْفِ جَرٍّ governor is a preposition → majrūr

2Case 1 — the governor requires a fāʿil رَفْعُ الْفَاعِلِيَّةِ

مَا حَضَرَ إِلَّا عَلِيٌّ "None were present except ʿAlī."
مَا Particle of negation, mabnī, no locus of iʿrāb.
حَضَرَ Māḍī verb, mabnī on a fatḥah — this is the governor (عَامِل), and by nature it requires a fāʿil.
إِلَّا Particle of exception, mabnī, no locus of iʿrāb — extra, not governing.
عَلِيٌّ Fāʿil of حَضَرَ, marfūʿ, sign of rafʿ the visible ḍammah — علي fills the subject slot that حضر always needs, exactly as it would if إلا were absent (مَا حَضَرَ عَلِيٌّ, "ʿAlī was not present," negated and then excepted).

3Case 2 — the governor requires a mafʿūl bihi نَصْبُ الْمَفْعُولِيَّةِ

مَا رَأَيْتُ إِلَّا عَلِيًّا "I did not see except ʿAlī."
مَا Particle of negation, mabnī, no locus of iʿrāb.
رَأَيْتُ Māḍī verb + attached fāʿil pronoun تُ ("I"), mabnī on sukūn in the position of rafʿ — رأيت is transitive, so its governor-nature demands a mafʿūl bihi.
إِلَّا Particle of exception, mabnī, no locus of iʿrāb.
عَلِيًّا Mafʿūl bihi of رَأَيْتُ, manṣūb, sign of naṣb the visible fatḥah — علياً fills the object slot رأيت requires, exactly as it would without إلا present.

4Case 3 — the governor is a preposition الْجَرُّ بِحَرْفِ جَرٍّ

مَا مَرَرْتُ إِلَّا بِزَيْدٍ "I did not pass except by Zayd."
مَا Particle of negation, mabnī, no locus of iʿrāb.
مَرَرْتُ Māḍī verb + attached fāʿil pronoun تُ ("I"), mabnī on sukūn in the position of rafʿ. مَرَّ is intransitive and takes its object via the preposition بِـ, so its governing demand here is for jarr, not naṣb.
إِلَّا Particle of exception, mabnī, no locus of iʿrāb.
بِزَيْدٍ بِ: particle of jarr, mabnī. زَيْدٍ: majrūr by بِ, sign of jarr the visible kasrah — the preposition بِـ is what governs زيد, entirely independent of إلا.

How to apply this rule quickly: mentally strip out إلا (and the negation, if you like) and ask what the bare sentence would need — حَضَرَ عَلِيٌّ needs a fāʿil, رَأَيْتُ عَلِيًّا needs a mafʿūl bihi, مَرَرْتُ بِزَيْدٍ needs a majrūr after بِـ. Whatever the plain sentence would require is exactly what the word after إلا becomes once you put the negation and إلا back in. Nothing about إلا itself changes the case here — it is the pre-existing governor (حضر / رأيت / مررت بـ) doing all the work.

In short — the three states, side by side: when the sentence before إلا is complete and affirmative, the mustathnā is always manṣūb (State 1). When it is complete and negative, the mustathnā may follow the mustathnā minhu's own case as a badal, or take naṣb, though naṣb here is rare (State 2). When it is incomplete — and therefore always negative — إلا drops out of the case-assignment picture altogether, and the word after it simply takes whatever case the sentence's real governor (a verb needing a fāʿil, a transitive verb needing a mafʿūl bihi, or a preposition) would assign it regardless (State 3).
