Recall from the earlier chapter that غَيْرُ، سِوَى، سُوَى، and سَوَاءٌ belong to the "always a noun" family of exception-tools. Because they are genuine nouns, they behave completely differently from إلا: instead of governing the noun that follows them into naṣb, they take that noun as a possessed term (muḍāf ilayh) — and it is the tool itself, not the following noun, that carries the exception's iʿrāb.

1The core rule الْقَاعِدَةُ الْأَسَاسِيَّةُ

Ibn Ājurrūm states it in one line: وَالْمُسْتَثْنَى بِـ سِوَى، وَسُوَى، وَسَوَاءٍ، وَغَيْرِ مَجْرُورٌ لَا غَيْرُ — "the word made an exception with siwā, suwā, sawāʾin, and ghayri is majrūr, nothing else." The commentary explains why there is no variation here at all:

الِاسْمُ بَعْدَ الْأَدَاةِ the noun after the tool always مجرور بالإضافة — majrūr, as muḍāf ilayh
الْأَدَاةُ نَفْسُهَا (غَيْر/سِوَى/سُوَى/سَوَاء) the tool itself takes the SAME three-state ruling as the noun after إلا

In other words, the "movement" that used to happen to the excepted noun directly after إلا (naṣb / badal / governor-dependent) now happens one level up, to the tool غير (or its sisters) — while the noun that follows the tool is permanently locked into jarr, because it is grammatically just the muḍāf ilayh of an iḍāfah compound.

2The three states, transposed onto غير أَحْوَالُ الْأَدَاةِ الثَّلَاثَة

state 1 — تام موجب وُجُوبُ النَّصْبِ غير obligatorily manṣūb
state 2 — تام منفي إِتْبَاعٌ أَوْ نَصْبٌ غير may follow (match case) or be manṣūb
state 3 — ناقص منفي حَسَبَ الْعَامِلِ غير takes whatever case the governor needs

3State 1 — complete & affirmative تَامٌّ مُوجَبٌ

قَامَ الْقَوْمُ غَيْرَ زَيْدٍ "The people stood, except Zayd." — the mustathnā minhu (القوم) is present, and nothing negates قام, so غير must be manṣūb, exactly as زيدًا was obligatorily manṣūb in the parallel إلا sentence.
قَامَ Māḍī verb, mabnī on a fatḥah.
الْقَوْمُ Fāʿil, marfūʿ — the mustathnā minhu.
غَيْرَ Mustathnā, obligatorily manṣūb, sign of naṣb the visible fatḥah — takes the exact ruling زيدًا took after إلا in State 1.
زَيْدٍ Muḍāf ilayh of غَيْرَ, majrūr, sign of jarr the visible kasrah — this is the fixed part of the rule that never changes across any of the three states.

4State 2 — complete & negative تَامٌّ مَنْفِيٌّ

مَا يَزُورُنِي أَحَدٌ غَيْرُ الْأَخْيَارِ / غَيْرَ الْأَخْيَارِ "No one visits me, except (ghayru/ghayra) the best [people]." — both غَيْرُ (marfūʿ) and غَيْرَ (manṣūb) are valid, mirroring the زيدٌ/زيدًا choice from State 2 of the إلا chapter.
غَيْرُreading 1 — al-badl
Follows أَحَدٌ (the mustathnā minhu) as a بَدَل, taking the same case: marfūʿ, sign of rafʿ the visible ḍammah.
غَيْرَreading 2 — al-istithnāʾ
Treated as an ordinary mustathnā, manṣūb, sign of naṣb the visible fatḥah — permissible, though (as with زيدًا earlier) the less common of the two options in real usage.

iʿrāb (reading 1 — غَيْرُ, badal)

مَا Particle of negation, mabnī.
يَزُورُنِي Muḍāriʿ verb, marfūʿ (visible ḍammah, implied not shown before the pronoun) with the attached object pronoun ـِي ("me"), mafʿūl bihi, mabnī in the position of naṣb.
أَحَدٌ Fāʿil of يَزُورُنِي, marfūʿ — and the مُسْتَثْنَى مِنْهُ.
غَيْرُ Badal from أَحَدٌ, marfūʿ following it, sign of rafʿ the visible ḍammah.
الْأَخْيَارِ Muḍāf ilayh of غَيْرُ, majrūr, sign of jarr the visible kasrah — fixed, as always.

5State 3 — incomplete, always negative نَاقِصٌ مَنْفِيٌّ

لَا تَتَّصِلْ بِـغَيْرِ الْأَخْيَارِ "Do not connect yourself except to the best [people]." — the mustathnā minhu is never mentioned, so غَيْرِ simply takes whatever case its governor (here, the preposition بِـ) requires.
لَا Particle of prohibition (حَرْفُ نَهْيٍ), mabnī — this is "shibh al-nafy," confirming the sentence is negative.
تَتَّصِلْ Muḍāriʿ verb, majzūm by لَا النَّاهِيَة, sign of jazm the sukūn. The implied فاعل is أَنْتَ ("you").
بِـ Particle of jarr, mabnī — this is the governor (عَامِل) here, and by nature it demands jarr.
غَيْرِ Majrūr by بِـ, sign of jarr the visible kasrah — غير simply takes the jarr that بِـ would assign any ordinary noun, exactly as the rule for State 3 requires.
الْأَخْيَارِ Muḍāf ilayh of غَيْرِ, majrūr, sign of jarr the visible kasrah — fixed, as always.

In short: غير، سوى، سُوى، and سواء are always nouns, so the word they introduce is always majrūr as their muḍāf ilayh — that part never changes across any context. What does change is the iʿrāb of the tool word itself, and it changes in exactly the same three-way pattern already established for the noun after إلا: obligatory naṣb in complete affirmative speech, a badal/naṣb choice in complete negative speech, and full dependence on the sentence's own governor in incomplete (always negative) speech.
