The third of the fifteen manṣūb positions — and the first of a pair (time and place) covered together "due to their similarities in ruling," as the commentary's own footnote explains, even though each gets its own precise definition so the two don't blur together for a beginner.

1What "ẓarf" means مَعْنَى الظَّرْف

Linguistically, ظَرْف just means "container." In grammatical use it's the technical name for الْمَفْعُولُ فِيهِ — literally "that which the action occurs in/at" — and it comes in two kinds: ظَرْفُ الزَّمَان (time) and ظَرْفُ الْمَكَان (place). This chapter covers the time half in full.

2The test: does "فِي" fit? مُلَاحَظَةُ مَعْنَى «فِي»

A time-word is only a true ẓarf when the action is understood to happen within it — i.e. "فِي" (in/during) could be inserted before it without changing the meaning. The same word can fail this test in a different sentence:

ظرف — passes the test صُمْتُ يَوْمَ الِاثْنَيْنِ "I fasted [in/on] Monday." — the fasting happened within that day
NOT ظرف — fails the test يَخَافُ الْكَسُولُ يَوْمَ الِامْتِحَانِ "The lazy student fears exam day." — he fears the day itself, not something happening in it

Same phrase-shape (يَوْمَ + noun), same case (both manṣūb) — but only the first is functioning as a true ẓarf zamān. The second is a manṣūb noun for a different reason (the object of "fears"), and the "فِي" test is exactly how you tell them apart.

3Specified vs. unspecified الْمُخْتَصُّ وَالْمُبْهَم

مُخْتَصّ a definite, bounded measure of time الشَّهْر، السَّنَة، الْيَوْم، الْعَام، الْأُسْبُوع
مُبْهَم an indefinite, unbounded measure اللَّحْظَة، الْوَقْت، الزَّمَان، الْحِين

Both kinds are equally valid as manṣūb ẓarf zamān — the distinction is about the word's own precision, not its grammatical eligibility.

4The twelve named time-words, on a schematic day الْأَلْفَاظُ الِاثْنَا عَشَرَ

Seven of the twelve sit on the daily cycle of dawn, sunrise, midday, sunset, and midnight. Laid out in order (not to scale):

طُلُوعُ الْفَجْرِdawn طُلُوعُ الشَّمْسِsunrise الزَّوَالmidday غُرُوبُ الشَّمْسِsunset نِصْفُ اللَّيْلِmidnight
سَحَرًا — end of night, just before dawn غَدْوَةً — fajr prayer to sunrise بُكْرَةً — start of daytime صَبَاحًا — mid-night to midday مَسَاءً — midday to midnight عَتَمَةً — first third of the night
الْيَوْمُ — all of dawn-to-sunset اللَّيْلَةُ — all of sunset-to-dawn

Schematic, not to scale — the point is the order of these anchors and which named period sits between which two. اليوم and الليلة are the two large halves; the other five are narrower windows nested within them.

5All twelve, in full — definitions and examples

1الْيَوْمُ
dawn to sunset
صُمْتُ الْيَوْمَ صُمْتُ يَوْمَ الْخَمِيسِ صُمْتُ يَوْمًا طَوِيلًا
2اللَّيْلَةُ
sunset to dawn
اعْتَكَفْتُ اللَّيْلَةَ الْبَارِحَةَ اعْتَكَفْتُ لَيْلَةً اعْتَكَفْتُ لَيْلَةَ الْجُمُعَةِ
3غَدْوَةً
between the fajr prayer and sunrise
زَارَنِي صَدِيقِي غَدْوَةَ الْأَحَدِ زَارَنِي غَدْوَةً
4بُكْرَةً
the very start of the daytime
أَزُورُكَ بُكْرَةَ السَّبْتِ أَزُورُكَ بُكْرَةً
5سَحَرًا
the end of the night, just before dawn
ذَاكَرْتُ دَرْسِي سَحَرًا
6غَدًا
the day after today
إِذَا جِئْتَنِي غَدًا أَكْرَمْتُكَ
7عَتَمَةً
the first third of the night
سَأَزُورُكَ عَتَمَةً
8صَبَاحًا
from the start of the night's second half to midday (al-zawāl)
سَافَرَ أَخِي صَبَاحًا
9مَسَاءً
from midday (al-zawāl) to midnight
وَصَلَ الْقِطَارُ بِنَا مَسَاءً
10أَبَدًا
unending future time
لَا أُصَاحِبُ الْأَشْرَارَ أَبَدًا
11أَمَدًا
unending future time (same sense as أبدا)
لَا أَقْتَرِفُ الشَّرَّ أَمَدًا
12حِينًا
a vague stretch of time, start and end unknown
صَاحَبْتُ عَلِيًّا حِينًا مِنَ الدَّهْرِ

A quirk worth flagging: أَبَدًا and its synonym أَمَدًا can mean "always/forever," but in a negated sentence — as in both examples above — they flip to meaning "never," precisely because they describe an unbounded stretch and the negation particle (لا) applies across that entire stretch.

6Beyond the twelve — anything alike joins the list

The named twelve aren't exhaustive — any noun indicating time, specified or unspecified, can be made manṣūb as ẓarf zamān the same way:

CategoryFurther examples
مختص (specified)ضَحْوَةً، ضُحًى
مبهم (unspecified)وَقْت، سَاعَة، لَحْظَة، زَمَان، بُرْهَة

In short: a ẓarf zamān is a manṣūb time-noun where the action can be understood as happening "فِي" it — the same word can fail this test in a different sentence (يوم الامتحان). Every valid ẓarf zamān is either مختص (a bounded, named measure like الشهر) or مُبهم (an unbounded, vague one like الوقت), and the author's twelve named examples — most of them anchored to specific points in the daily cycle — are just the most common instances of a category that any time-word can join.
