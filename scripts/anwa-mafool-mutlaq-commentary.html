This chapter directly resolves something flagged in the previous one: why جَذْلًا could stand in for a "matching" maṣdar despite sharing no letters with فَرِحْتُ. The answer turns out to be a whole second classification, orthogonal to the emphasis/manner/number split — the مفعول مطلق is also either لَفْظِيّ (verbal) or مَعْنَوِيّ (abstract).

1The two types, at a glance

لَفْظِيّ matches the verb in both letters and meaning
مَعْنَوِيّ matches the verb in meaning only — different letters

The commentary notes the لفظي type is listed first specifically because it's the more common of the two.

2لَفْظِيّ — matching in letters and meaning

The maṣdar shares the verb's own root letters, and naturally its meaning too:

قَتَلْتُق ت ل
=
قَتْلًاق ت ل
قَتَلْتُهُ قَتْلًا"I fought/killed him — a [real] fight/killing."
قَعَدْتُ قُعُودًا"I sat — a sitting."
ضَرَبْتُهُ ضَرْبًا"I hit him — a hitting."
ذَهَبْتُ ذِهَابًا"I went — a going."

3مَعْنَوِيّ — matching in meaning only

The maṣdar's letters are entirely unrelated to the verb's own root — the connection is purely semantic:

جَلَسْتُج ل س
≠
قُعُودًاق ع د — same meaning, different root
جَلَسْتُ قُعُودًا"I sat — a sitting." (جلس means the same as قعد)
فَرِحْتُ جَذْلًا"I rejoiced — a jubilation."
ضَرَبْتُهُ لَكْمًا"I hit him — a punch." (لكم = a specific kind of striking)
أَهَنْتُهُ احْتِقَارًا"I demeaned him — a belittling."
قُمْتُ وُقُوفًا"I stood up — a standing."

This is exactly what was happening with فَرِحْتُ بِقُدُومِكَ جَذْلًا from the previous chapter: جَذْلًا was never meant to share فَرِحْتُ's root — it's a straightforward مَعْنَوِيّ مفعول مطلق, the second of these two types, doing precisely what the definition says it should.

4Two dimensions at once تَقَاطُعُ التَّصْنِيفَيْنِ

This لفظي/معنوي split doesn't replace the earlier emphasis/manner/number classification — it sits alongside it. Every مفعول مطلق can be placed on both dimensions simultaneously:

Exampleلفظي or معنوي؟Function (from the previous chapter)
حَفِظْتُ الدَّرْسَ حِفْظًالفظيemphasis
فَرِحْتُ بِقُدُومِكَ جَذْلًامعنويemphasis
وَقَفْتُ لِلْأُسْتَاذِ وُقُوفَ الْمُؤَدَّبِلفظيmanner
ضَرَبْتُ الْكَسُولَ ضَرْبَتَيْنِلفظيnumber

In other words, "emphasis/manner/number" answers what job the مفعول مطلق is doing in the sentence; "لفظي/معنوي" answers how closely its wording tracks the verb. The two questions are independent — knowing one doesn't tell you the other.

In short: a مفعول مطلق is لفظي when its letters match the verb's own root (قَتَلْتُ قَتْلًا), and مَعْنَوِيّ when only the meaning matches, drawing on an entirely different root (جَلَسْتُ قُعُودًا، فَرِحْتُ جَذْلًا). This is a separate axis from the earlier three-way functional split, and the two combine freely — resolving, in passing, exactly why جَذْلًا was a legitimate example even without sharing a single letter with فَرِحْتُ.
